What SpaceView is

Android-first (Google Play) AR app: measure anything precisely; scan an object (e.g. photo frame in a café) → save to "AR Gallery" with exact dimensions → place it in your room at true size. Later: B2B for Indian retailers (Croma, Reliance Digital, furniture), iOS (ARKit/LiDAR). Original brief: user's pasted spec (8→9 agents, orchestrator = Claude Opus 5.5). Accuracy is #1 priority. Time & space (size/perf) optimisation required for Play.

Status: research / planning only (no app code). Prepared 2026-11-02. Scope: how accurately phones can measure, how to get the most accuracy out of them, how to capture objects for the "AR Gallery", which stack to use, how many Indian phones are covered, and what the measurement engine should look like.

Bottom line. On a typical Indian mid-range ARCore phone without a depth sensor, an honest promise for lengths of 0.5–3 m is about ±2% of the length, and never better than ±1 cm (roughly ±1–2 cm at 1 m, ±3–6 cm at 3 m) under good conditions. Error gets worse with distance, featureless walls, dim light and fast motion. "±1 cm at 1 m" is a fair claim only for LiDAR iPhones/iPads (and possibly ToF Android phones), on textured surfaces, with careful technique. Do not use it in Android marketing until our own test protocol (section 6) proves it on specific devices.

For v1, use native Kotlin + ARCore SDK, rendering with SceneView/ARSceneView (Filament). Isolate the measurement core so it does not depend on the renderer. Capture objects with a "measured proxy": measured W×H×D plus a perspective-corrected photo texture, built into a textured box/plane glTF in metres. Do not use photogrammetry for v1. Later, iOS should be native Swift ARKit + RealityKit (built on GitHub Actions macOS runners), with LiDAR and Object Capture as an optional "high-fidelity" mode.

1.3 How error grows with distance

There are three separate effects:

  1. Pixel / endpoint localisation, which grows linearly with distance Z. One image pixel covers about Z / f_px. With ARCore's default 640×480 CPU image and ~65° horizontal FOV, f ≈ 500 px, so 1 px ≈ 2 mm at 1 m, 4 mm at 2 m, 6 mm at 3 m. A finger tap on a phone screen is accurate to several camera pixels at best. This is why we need a magnifier loupe, edge snapping, and a high-resolution CPU image stream (CameraConfig) where the device supports one.
  2. Depth / plane-distance bias, which acts as a proportional error. If the surface is estimated δZ too far away, every length on it is scaled by (1 + δZ/Z). A 1 cm plane bias at 1 m means 1% length error. Monocular depth bias grows roughly with Z², so it gets worse fast beyond 2 m. LiDAR is roughly constant within 0.3–3 m (see the PMC table).
  3. VIO scale error and drift, which grow with path length and time, not with Z directly. A short, still measurement taken from one viewpoint has little drift. Walking 4 m along a wall to measure it builds up drift (Sensors 2022: ~2 cm/s of movement in hard cases).

Rule of thumb: relative error is roughly constant (≈1–3%) when the whole object is in view from one spot. It rises when (a) you are > 2.5–3 m away (pixel and depth limits), (b) you must walk to see both endpoints (drift), or (c) the surface is low-texture.

1.5 Honest claims we can make

  • Android (non-ToF): "Typically within ±2% (about ±1–2 cm at arm's length) in good light on textured surfaces. Each measurement shows its own confidence range." Promise nothing better until our protocol shows it per device tier.
  • LiDAR iPhone: "Typically within ±1 cm up to ~2 m" is supported by Luetzenburg 2021 and PMC10939328, but still must be verified for our own pipeline.
  • Never claim "laser-accurate". Laser distance meters are ±1.5–2 mm. Apple itself calls Measure "approximate", and Google withdrew its own Measure app.
  • Always display value ± uncertainty plus a quality badge, and refuse to save "precise" records when the uncertainty is above a threshold.

2. Techniques to maximise accuracy

Listed in rough order of payoff:

  1. Tracking-quality gating (mandatory). Accept samples only when Camera.trackingState == TRACKING and trackingFailureReason == NONE. Also gate on gyro angular velocity (reject motion blur, e.g. > ~0.3 rad/s), on scene brightness (ARCore light estimate / mean pixel intensity), and on feature count in the ROI. Make an initialisation phase mandatory: a "move the phone side to side" prompt until tracking is stable and a plane is found, because parallax gives VIO its scale.
  2. Hit-test priority and filtering. Prefer, in order: (a) Plane hits with isPoseInPolygon on a TRACKING plane that is not subsumed; (b) DepthPoint hits (Depth API enabled, raw-depth confidence above threshold); (c) feature Point hits with a surface normal. Never accept InstantPlacementPoint for measurements. Hit results come back sorted by distance; take the first valid one of the preferred type.
  3. Own plane fitting near the target. Don't trust ARCore's plane polygon alone. Collect raw-depth points (acquireRawDepthImage16Bits / acquireRawDepthImageMeters from ARCore 1.56, with confidence ≥ ~200/255) plus ARCore feature points inside the region of interest. Fit a plane with RANSAC + least squares and project tapped or snapped 2D points onto it by ray–plane intersection. This is the key technique for wall-hung frames and TVs. It also gives a plane-fit residual that feeds the uncertainty estimate.
  4. Multi-sample averaging per endpoint. While the reticle is held on a point, collect N ≈ 15–30 samples over ~0.5–1 s, ideally with a small parallax sweep. Use a robust estimator (median or trimmed mean). The sample spread gives a per-endpoint covariance. Report σ and reject if it is above a threshold.
  5. Anchor endpoints and recompute at save time. Create an Anchor per endpoint. ARCore refines anchor poses as its map improves, so compute final distances from current anchor poses, and recompute when the user taps "save". Show the value converging ("stabilising…").
  6. Edge and corner snapping (computer vision). Detect line segments and quadrilaterals in the camera image around the reticle: OpenCV LSD/Canny + Hough, or a contour-quad detector for frames and TVs. Snap to sub-pixel corners (cornerSubPix). Ask for the high-resolution CPU stream via Session.getSupportedCameraConfigs() / setCameraConfig() (default is VGA 640×480). Rectangle mode: detect the 4 corners of a frame or TV, then intersect each corner ray with the fitted wall plane to get W and H in one shot. Enforce right angles and parallel sides as a consistency check (deviation feeds uncertainty).
  7. Distance coaching. Show live guidance for the target's best distance: "move closer" when > 2 m for objects under 1 m; "step back" when the object doesn't fit in the frame. Get the whole object into one view so there is no walking drift. Recommend a near-perpendicular view to the surface (< 30° obliquity), because oblique views increase depth bias.
  8. Scale-drift handling. For spans that need walking (room dimensions, long walls), use an anchor chain: drop intermediate anchors, keep the path short, and encourage a loop back to the start to trigger loop closure. Show a larger uncertainty. Above ~5 m, recommend a laser meter or "measure in segments".
  9. Reference-object calibration (optional "precision mode"). The user puts a known object on the same plane as the target and measures it: A4 sheet 297 × 210 mm, or ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 card 85.60 × 53.98 mm. The scale factor s = true/measured corrects VIO scale for that plane and session. Apply it only if 0.9 < s < 1.1, otherwise flag a tracking problem. Caveats: a card is too small at distance (at 1.5 m it spans ~30 VGA px, so a 1 px error ≈ 3%). Use A4 for anything beyond ~50 cm, and use the high-res stream. The same reference supports a single-photo homography mode (no AR needed): with a full-res still (f ≈ 3000 px), the A4's 4 corners define the plane's metric homography, and the target's corners on that plane are measured directly. Error grows when extrapolating to objects much larger than the reference, so give it an uncertainty from corner-localisation error propagation. This mode is also the fallback for non-ARCore phones (section 5).
  10. Uncertainty estimation shown to the user. Propagate per-endpoint covariance, plane-fit residual, a distance-dependent pixel term, a device-tier scale prior (from our test data), and a tracking-quality penalty. Show "60.4 cm ± 0.9 cm". Colour-code: green ≤1%, amber ≤3%, red >3%. Calibrate the displayed ± in the test protocol so that ~90% of true values really fall inside the band. A ± that is honest is worth more than one that is small.
  11. Repeat-and-agree. For "save to Gallery", require 2 independent measurements (e.g. re-aim) that agree within the shown uncertainty. Save the mean.
  12. Environment hints. Warn on low light, plain white walls (low feature count), glass, mirrors and glossy black TV screens. For TVs, snap to the bezel or outer frame, not the screen reflection.

3.2 Why the proxy wins for frames, TVs and appliances

  • These objects are planar or box-shaped. A box with exact W×H×D and good front-face texture is visually convincing when placed against a wall, and it is dimensionally exact by construction.
  • Photogrammetry is weakest on exactly these objects: glass in frames, glossy black TV panels, uniform white appliance panels. These give holes and wobbly edges, so the mesh's bounding box differs from the true size by more than the measurement error. Cropping a mesh and estimating a bounding box adds more error.
  • What the user cares about is "does it fit my wall / cabinet". That is the bounding box. A proxy gives the bounding box directly; photogrammetry gives it only indirectly.
  • It needs no server, no per-scan cost, and works offline (good for Indian connectivity), with an instant result.

3.3 Recommended capture pipeline (v1 Android)

  1. Detect the rectangle (CV quad + AR plane) and measure W×H on the fitted plane, as in section 2.
  2. Measure depth D:
    • Wall-hung (frame, TV): offer presets (thin frame 2 cm, canvas 4 cm, TV ~ model lookup) or a side-view 2-point measurement. Mark D as "low confidence", because a 2–5 cm protrusion is comparable to the error on non-depth phones.
    • Free-standing (appliance): 3-point box. Two base points on the floor plane give W and D, and a vertical height point gives H.
  3. Grab a high-res still (the ARCore CPU image at max config, or a CameraX still if switching is acceptable). Unwarp the front face with the homography from the 4 detected corners into a straight-on texture (e.g. 1024 px on the long side). Optionally capture side and top textures, or use solid colour sampled from the edges.
  4. Build the model. Front face = texture, sides = edge colour, optional bevel. Real-world units 1 unit = 1 m. Save as glTF 2.0 / GLB (Scene Viewer / Filament native). Keep it under 10 MB and under 50k triangles.
  5. Save the canonical record, not just the mesh: {dims_m, uncertainty_m, per-dim confidence, device model, ARCore version, tracking stats, calibration used, textures, capture photos, timestamps}. Meshes are generated from this record per platform (GLB on Android, USDZ on iOS), so later re-export or upgrade is possible.

3.4 Later

  • iOS LiDAR "High-fidelity scan" mode with Object Capture for real 3D objects (vases, chairs). Snap its mesh's bounding box to the measured proxy dims, or show both.
  • Android: optional cloud photogrammetry for 3D objects, using ARCore poses to fix scale. Only after the proxy flow is proven.

4. Stack recommendation

4.1 Android (v1)

Recommended: native Kotlin + ARCore SDK (com.google.ar:core, current 1.57.0, Oct 2026) + SceneView arsceneview (current ~4.65.0; Jetpack Compose + Filament; Apache-2.0; Android "Stable").

  • Sceneform is deprecated (Google archived it in 2020). SceneView is its community successor and the de facto current choice. It needs minSdk 24, compileSdk 37, AGP 9.4+, Kotlin 2.4, JVM target 21. This matches the dev's JDK 21 CLI + Gradle + GitHub Actions setup with no Android Studio.
    • Risk: SceneView is largely one maintainer (Thomas Gorisse) and has fast-moving versions. Mitigation: keep the measurement engine renderer-agnostic. It talks to the ARCore Session/Frame directly, and SceneView is only the view layer. If SceneView stalls, swap to raw Filament or OpenGL ES.
  • Raw ARCore + OpenGL ES (Google's hello_ar_kotlin sample pattern) gives full control and has no third-party dependency, but means writing your own rendering, glTF loading and occlusion. It is a viable fallback, but slower to build for the Gallery/placement features.
  • ARCore for Jetpack XR (androidx.xr.arcore, still alpha) runs on phones through Play Services for AR, but lacks some features and is aimed at Android XR headsets. Not for v1.
  • Unity AR Foundation is ruled out (no Unity, heavy licence and tooling), and has no accuracy advantage: it wraps the same ARCore/ARKit.
  • Scene Viewer (Google's intent-based AR viewer, glTF/GLB, resizable=false locks true scale, mode=ar_only) is a zero-code way to "place in room at true scale" for prototyping or as a fallback viewer. However, it needs ARCore and the Google app, and only offers basic UX. Use it for the early demo, then build in-app placement with SceneView.
  • Computer vision: OpenCV Android SDK (LSD, contours, cornerSubPix, homography) inside a pure-Kotlin/JNI module.

4.2 iOS (later)

Native Swift + ARKit + RealityKit, built and signed on GitHub Actions macOS runners (no Mac locally). Use:

  • ARWorldTrackingConfiguration with .sceneDepth / .smoothedSceneDepth on LiDAR devices, and raycasts for non-LiDAR devices.
  • Vision VNDetectRectanglesRequest for frame and TV quads.
  • RoomPlan for room outline (with the caveat about its accuracy).
  • ObjectCaptureSession for the LiDAR high-fidelity mode.
  • Quick Look / RealityKit to place USDZ.

The math core (filters, plane fit, uncertainty, record schema) could be shared through Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP builds an XCFramework on a macOS runner). Alternatively, port it as a small, well-tested Swift module. Either way, the test vectors (recorded inputs and expected outputs) are what must be shared.

  • SceneView's iOS port is still Alpha, so don't rely on it for iOS.

4.3 What "REA" probably means

There is no mainstream AR framework called "REA". Plausible readings, most likely first:

  1. Apple's "Reality" stack: RealityKit / Reality Composer (Pro) / .reality files. This is the most natural reading of "use REA if needed" in an AR app that will later go to iOS. Best guess. It maps to "use RealityKit on iOS".
  2. React Native AR, i.e. ViroReact (ReactVision, maintained in 2025, New-Architecture support, ARCore + ARKit), or "REA" as the class prefix of React Native Reanimated. That would imply a cross-platform JS UI. For a precision measurement engine this adds a bridge layer and hides ARCore/ARKit details we need (raw depth, camera configs, anchors). It is acceptable only for non-AR UI screens.
  3. Google's Scene Viewer / <model-viewer> (web AR viewer), if "REA" was a typo for a viewer component.
  4. University of Ulm's "AREA" research framework (location-based AR). Unlikely.

Action: confirm with the user. Whatever it means, the measurement engine should stay native.

6.3 Accuracy test protocol

Ground truth

  • Reference instrument: laser distance meter (±1.5 mm class) plus a steel tape / 1 m steel rule. Measure each GT object 3×, use the mean, and record temperature-independent values.
  • Object set (keep it physically in a "test kit" box):
    • A4 sheet 297×210 mm; ID-1 card 85.60×53.98 mm
    • 2 photo frames (e.g. 30×40 cm and 50×70 cm), one glossy glass, one matte
    • A TV (e.g. 43", ~96×56 cm outer) switched off (glossy black)
    • A box appliance (microwave ~45×26×35 cm) and a cardboard box with known dims
    • A door (~0.9×2.1 m) and 2 wall lengths (~3 m, ~5 m) for room-span mode
    • Height: a person/pole of known height (~1.7 m)

Conditions matrix (subset per release, full matrix per milestone)

  • Distance: 0.5, 1, 2, 3 m. View angle: 0°, 30°, 45°.
  • Light: bright (>500 lux), home evening (~100–150 lux), mixed or backlit (window).
  • Surface: textured wall, plain white/painted wall, glossy surface.
  • Devices (tiers): T0 reference Pixel; T1 Depth-API mid-rangers (Redmi Note, Galaxy A5x, realme, vivo/iQOO, moto g); T2 non-Depth budget ARCore phone; T3 LiDAR iPhone Pro (iOS phase); T4 non-LiDAR iPhone (iOS phase).
  • Repetitions: 10 per condition × 3 operators (including one first-time user, to capture UX-driven error).

Metrics

  • Signed error (bias), absolute error (mm), relative error (%), median, P95, repeatability (SD of repeats), between-operator reproducibility.
  • Uncertainty calibration: fraction of trials where GT lies inside the displayed ± band (target 85–95%; too high means the band is too wide to be useful).
  • Reject/abstain rate: the share of attempts where the engine refused or warned. Low error achieved by refusing everything is a fail.
  • Time-to-measure.

Pure :measure-core logic gets JVM unit tests with synthetic data in GitHub Actions.
  • Keep a versioned GT catalogue (JSON) and generate an HTML/CSV report per build.

6.4 Open risks

  1. Plain painted walls (very common): vertical planes may not be detected, and depth-from-motion is weak there. This hits the core "frame on café wall" use case. Mitigations: rely on the frame's own texture and edges for depth points, 3-tap plane, reference-sheet mode.
  2. Scale error on budget phones with poor IMU calibration. Is the certified list's quality enough for 1.5%? Unknown until tested. Calibration mode helps.
  3. Depth (D) of flat objects cannot be measured reliably without LiDAR/ToF.
  4. SceneView bus factor (single maintainer, very frequent breaking upgrades of AGP/Kotlin/compileSdk). Isolate it and pin versions.
  5. Unknown ARCore coverage share in India. Instrument from day 1, and keep the non-AR fallback first-class.
  6. Glossy and transparent surfaces (glass frames, TV screens) cause bad hits. Snap to bezels and frames, and warn.
  7. iOS without a Mac: signing, provisioning and on-device testing via GitHub Actions + TestFlight only; slow iteration. LiDAR test devices are needed for T3 validation.
  8. Expectation management: competitor apps claim "1 cm accuracy". Our honest ± display may look worse in screenshots, but it is the defensible position. Google withdrew its own Measure app over accuracy complaints.

0. TL;DR: the 12 decisions that matter most

  1. Be honest about precision. On mid-range Android phones without depth sensors, phone AR gives an estimate. Every measurement shows a ± tolerance and a 3-level quality badge (Good / Fair / Rough), and we round the number to match the tolerance (we never show "42.37 cm ± 1 cm"). Every serious competitor hedges: Apple calls its results approximate, CamToPlan says "accuracy not guaranteed", and Magicplan says accuracy "depends" [7][14][13].
  2. Use a centre-fixed crosshair, not tap-to-place points, for measuring. You move the phone and press a big bottom-centre "+" button. This is the Apple Measure pattern. It works one-handed and avoids fingers covering the target.
  3. Offer reference-card calibration (any bank card or PVC Aadhaar card is the ID-1 size, 85.60 × 53.98 mm; an A4 sheet also works) as an optional "Boost accuracy" step. This is our main lever for "high precision" on phones without LiDAR or ToF sensors.
  4. The scan → save flow captures a measured box plus a straightened front photo (a "card" or "box" proxy), not a full 3D mesh, in v1 on Android. This is fast, small and works offline. Full photogrammetry can be a later, cloud-assisted mode (Polycam on Android is photo-mode only [10]).
  5. Placing an object always uses true scale, with scaling locked. Apple's HIG says that letting shoppers scale furniture "doesn't help them visualize" [3]. Show a "1:1 True size" lock chip.
  6. Give a fit verdict that includes uncertainty: "Fits, 6 cm to spare", "Tight: check with a tape", or "Too big by 4 cm". This verdict is the core value of the product.
  7. Wall placement needs a fallback. Indian homes often have plain, single-colour painted walls, and Google warns that such vertical surfaces often fail detection [2]. Offer "mark where the wall meets the floor" so we can build the wall plane from the floor plane.
  8. Never show an ad inside a camera or AR view. The interstitial appears only after the "Saved" result screen, when the user taps Done. It never appears when the user taps "Place in room". The first scan in a session is ad-free, and there is a time-based cap (AdMob allows at most one interstitial per two user actions [5]).
  9. Banners appear only on list screens without a camera (Gallery, History, Settings), never next to the shutter or navigation controls. AdMob names "gallery image" and "next" buttons as risky neighbours [15]. Partner catalogue pages carry no AdMob ads.
  10. Share to WhatsApp first: an annotated spec image ("Frame 45.7 × 61.0 cm (18 × 24 in)") plus a link. That is how measurements actually travel in India.
  11. Units are context-aware: feet and inches for rooms and people, cm or inches for objects, and inches on the diagonal for TVs and monitors (Indian retail lists TVs as "108 cm (43 inch)"). Always show a secondary unit.
  12. Visual language is a precision instrument: drafting-style dimension lines, tabular numerals, a graphite and paper palette, and one high-visibility signal colour. No gradients, sparkles or "AI-powered" badges.

  • Apple Measure's crosshair plus "+" button. It works one-handed, keeps the target visible, and the reticle stays the same size wherever you aim [7].
  • Snapping to edges and corners with a visible "snap" state (Apple guides) [7]. On Android without depth, we can snap to plane boundaries, to existing points, and to rectangles detected by on-device edge detection.
  • Moasure's after-measurement coaching. It shows what went wrong and how to fix it, instead of a bare number [8].
  • Canvas and Magicplan's escape hatch: "for exact numbers, enter or verify critical dimensions manually" [13][23]. We should make manual override a first-class feature, not a failure.
  • Amazon's one-tap "View in your room" entry from product detail. Our gallery item pages need the same.

1.3 Pitfalls to avoid

  • Unqualified precision claims ("98% accurate") that users then disprove with a tape. This destroys trust and leads to 1-star reviews.
  • Low-light drift with no warning. IKEA Place users had to discover it themselves [17]. We should warn before placement when light is low.
  • Ads that interrupt measuring. This is the most common AR Ruler complaint [12].
  • Saved items buried in deep folders [18]. The gallery needs search, filters and thumbnails that show the dimensions.
  • Heavy, slow scanning (Kreativ's multi-minute capture and upload) [16]. The scan should take under 30 seconds and save on the device.

2.5 Comfort and safety

  • Don't make people walk backwards. This comes from Google's AR design guidelines, as summarised by a reviewer [25]. In practice, our flows always guide the user forward or sideways ("Step closer", "Move to the side"), never "step back". If an object needs more distance, we say "Move the phone back slightly", not "step back".
  • Comfort: holding a phone up for a long time is fatiguing [3]. Measuring sessions should be short. Allow Freeze frame: hold the phone up, tap the snowflake, then lower the phone and adjust points on the frozen image (the camera pose is kept). This is a big win for comfort and accuracy.
  • Placement range: ARCore recommends a default maximum placement distance, with "center stage" as the comfortable zone [2]. Place new objects 1–1.5 m in front of the user on the floor or wall.
  • Safety notice on first AR use: "Watch your surroundings: stairs, pets, traffic." Shown once, not modal on every launch.
  • Thermal and battery: pause the AR session and camera whenever a sheet or dialog covers more than half the screen, and on any non-AR screen.

2.6 Object interaction during placement (from the Apple HIG [3])

  • One-finger drag to move along the surface, two-finger twist to rotate. No pinch-scale (true scale is locked).
  • Respond to gestures near the object, not only on it.
  • Keep the object attached to the surface while dragging. Never let it jump or vanish.
  • For precision, show nudge arrows (±1 cm per tap, long-press to repeat) and a rotate 90° button in screen space, reachable by thumb. AR touch gestures are imprecise, and nudge controls also help one-handed use and accessibility.

3.3 Core flow: Scan → Save → Place

A. Scan (S4): about 20–40 s, 5 steps with a step indicator at the top ("2 of 4")

  1. Pick the type (one tap, which sets the capture recipe and the defaults): Flat on a wall (frame, mirror, TV, poster) · Box-shaped (cabinet, appliance, box) · Round (table, plant pot) · Something else. Wording note: the café-frame use case is "Flat on a wall".
  2. Find the surface. Coaching overlay, then the plane is found. For "Flat on a wall", the user aims at the wall; if the wall isn't found within 5 s, offer "Use the wall–floor line instead" (§3.5).
  3. Set the outline.
    • Flat: place two diagonal corners with the crosshair (the rectangle snaps to detected edges if available). The live W × H appears with ± and the diagonal (for TVs and monitors).
    • Box: tap the 3 base corners on the floor or table (giving W and D), then raise the height handle until it touches the top. A wireframe box appears, with dimension lines on each edge.
    • Round: tap the centre, then the edge (giving the diameter), then the height.
  4. Capture the appearance. "Step to face it straight on, then hold still": an auto-capture fires when the phone is roughly parallel (live tilt guide) and steady. For box items, an optional "Add side photo". We crop and straighten the photo using the measured outline, so the texture matches the dimensions exactly.
  5. Accuracy check (inline, not a separate screen). Show the quality badge. If the result is Fair or Rough, show one best tip with an action: "Measure again from 50 cm. Usually improves to ± 0.5 cm" [Re-measure] [Calibrate with card] [Keep as is].

B. Review (S6)

  • Dimension card: W 45.5 cm ± 0.5 · H 61.0 cm ± 0.5 · Diagonal 76.1 cm (30.0 in)
  • Each value is tappable: Edit number (keypad with a unit switch), Re-measure this side (back into AR for one edge only), or Lock as exact (for example, "the shop label says 45 × 60").
  • Auto-suggested name ("Wooden frame"; on-device image labelling can propose a category, but the user confirms it). An optional note and where ("Blue Tokai, Indiranagar", from manual entry; never auto-attach location without opt-in).
  • Primary button: Save to Gallery.

C. Saved (S7)

  • Confirmation (motion: the thumbnail flies into the Gallery tab), with three actions: [Place in my room] (primary) · [Share] · [Done]
  • Ad rule: an interstitial may show only when leaving via Done or the system Back gesture (end of a content segment), and never when the user chooses Place or Share (see §5).

D. Place (S10)

  1. Choose a destination type based on the item type: wall item → find wall; floor item → find floor; tabletop item → find table.
  2. A ghost footprint at true size follows the crosshair (with a soft contact shadow so it reads as grounded [3]). A "1:1 True size" chip is always visible.
  3. Tap to drop it. The object is integrated immediately and refined later (Apple: don't wait for perfect data [3]).
  4. Fit tools: clearance callouts to nearby detected planes or edges ("12 cm to the cupboard", "1.42 m above the floor"); for frames, the centre height shown against an "eye-level guide" option.
  5. Fit verdict, when the user measures a gap (e.g. wall space between two windows): Fits ✓ (6 cm spare) / Tight ⚠ (within the margin of error: confirm with a tape) / Too big ✕ (by 4 cm).
  6. Snapshot button: the photo is saved with dimension overlays and can be shared straight away.
  7. Compare: add a second gallery item (e.g. a Croma TV next to your own scanned cabinet).

3.4 Measure modes (S3)

Line (point to point) · Height (floor to point, using the floor plane plus a vertical guide) · Rectangle/Frame (auto-rectangle) · Box · Floor area (polygon, in sq ft / m²) · Path (multi-segment total). Defer angle, volume and level until after v1. AR Ruler-style mode overload contributes to confusion; a focused set is better.

3.5 Wall fallback (India-specific importance)

Plain, single-colour painted walls are common in Indian homes and rentals, and these are exactly the surfaces ARCore struggles with [2]. Fallback: (1) detect the floor (usually textured: tiles, rug); (2) the user aims the crosshair at the line where the wall meets the floor and taps two points along it; (3) we build a vertical wall plane through that line; (4) the frame can now be placed on this virtual wall. Copy: "Can't see the wall clearly. Tap two points where the wall meets the floor."

4. Communicating accuracy and uncertainty

4.1 Principles

  1. Every number carries its tolerance. Format: 45.5 cm ± 0.5. Show ± in the same unit as the value.
  2. Displayed precision follows the tolerance. If ± ≥ 1 cm, show whole cm; if ± < 1 cm, show 1 decimal place; never show mm unless ± ≤ 2 mm (only with card calibration at close range). In inches, round to 1/4" or 1/8" according to the tolerance. Showing false precision is the fastest way to lose trust.
  3. Use words and shape, not only colour. Error bars are poorly understood by non-experts [26], so pair a plain-language badge with the ±:
Badge Icon shape Rule of thumb Copy
Good Filled circle ● ± ≤ 0.5 cm, or ≤ 1% "Good: within about half a centimetre"
Fair Half circle ◐ ± ≤ 2 cm, or ≤ 3% "Fair: fine for planning, check before buying"
Rough Empty circle ○ anything worse "Rough estimate: move closer or add light"

(The thresholds are placeholders for the engineering team to calibrate against our own bench tests across target devices.) 4. Show the uncertainty in the AR view itself: render the dimension line as a thin sleeve (tolerance band) whose thickness at the end caps is proportional to ±. It shows imprecision visually without any text. 5. Explain the method on demand. Tap the badge for "How we measured: depth estimate, 3 samples, 0.7 m away, card-calibrated". This transparency is the opposite of "AI magic". 6. Coach after the fact, like Moasure [8]: name the single biggest cause ("You were 1.8 m away; under 1 m is best") with a one-tap fix.

4.2 Inputs to the uncertainty estimate (for engineering)

Distance to the target, tracking state and its recent history, plane confidence or plane age, whether depth is available (Depth API support varies by device and must be checked at runtime [27]), angle of incidence, number of repeat samples, card calibration on or off, and light level. Expose the model output as one ± per dimension.

4.3 Ways to improve accuracy (offered in this order)

  1. Move closer (target: 0.5–1 m).
  2. Re-measure and average: "Measure again from a different angle. We'll combine both." Show the spread of the samples.
  3. Calibrate with a card: place a bank card, PVC Aadhaar card or other ID-1 card (85.60 × 53.98 mm), or an A4 sheet, flat near the object; the app finds its outline and computes a scale correction. Badge: "Card-calibrated".
  4. Freeze and refine: freeze the frame, then drag the endpoints with the loupe.
  5. Enter a known value: "Know one side exactly? Enter it." For photo-based captures, the other sides can rescale proportionally (shown as "Scaled from your 45 cm").

4.4 User correction UX

  • Tap any dimension, in AR or on the review card, to open the Edit sheet: numeric field (with the unit switch), Re-measure this side, Reset to measured.
  • Edited values show a pencil marker and the provenance "Edited by you"; the original measured value is kept in item history.
  • Fit verdicts use the edited value but warn if it differs from the measured value by more than 3× the tolerance ("Your value differs from what we measured. Double-check?").
  • Undo is always one tap away in AR (bottom-left, in thumb reach), plus "Clear all".

4.5 Marketing and store copy (to stay credible)

Say "Measure in seconds, with an accuracy rating on every result" rather than "precise to the millimetre". Publish an accuracy page (S15) with honest numbers from our own tests, by device class.

5. Ad placement UX

5.1 Policy constraints that apply to SpaceView

  • Interstitials only at natural breaks between content, never during an active task; not on app load or exit; at most one interstitial per two user actions; never back-to-back; never on every click; must not block core content; pre-load to avoid an ad appearing late, after the next screen loads [5][11].
  • Play's disruptive-ads policy: "unexpected" means mid-task, or the user tapped a button expecting to start an activity and got an ad. Placement after a results screen (the "score screen") is expected [28]. Full-screen interstitials that can't be closed after 15 s are prohibited [29].
  • Banners: never next to interactive buttons ("next", menu bars, gallery images, gameplay); no floating or overlapping banners; accidental clicks lead to revenue deductions and suspension [15].
  • No ads on dead-end or no-content screens (thank-you, exit, error, loading), or in apps or screens where the user is looking away from the screen (the flashlight example) [30]. An AR viewfinder, where the user's attention is on the world, is closely analogous, which is another reason to keep ads out of camera views.
  • Frequency capping is available per ad unit and app-wide (e.g. "2 impressions per 30 minutes") [31].

5.2 Interstitial placement ("one per scan" without annoyance)

Scan (AR) ──> Review ──> Save ──> Saved card

                                   ├── [Share] ──> share sheet (NO AD)

Eligibility rules (all must be true):

  1. The user completed and saved a scan (a whole content segment is finished). Abandoned scans never trigger an ad.
  2. It is not the user's first scan of the session, and not their first-ever scan (a grace period for first impressions and onboarding).
  3. At least 3 minutes since the last interstitial (client-side), plus an AdMob unit cap of about 4 per hour as a backstop [31].
  4. The ad is already pre-loaded. If it is not ready, skip it: never show a spinner and then an ad.
  5. The user is not in "Place", has not just tapped Share, and has no system dialog open.
  6. Deferred placement: if the user taps "Place in my room", the owed ad is carried over to the next natural exit (closing the AR placement screen back to the Gallery), and only once.

Plus:

  • Pause and release the AR session and camera before the ad shows (Firebase: pause the action during an interstitial [11]), and give the ad the full screen.
  • After the ad closes, land on the expected screen (Gallery, with the new item highlighted), so the ad feels like a pause, not a detour.
  • Rewarded option: "Watch an ad to unlock 24 h ad-free" or "...to unlock HD export". Opt-in ads are explicitly not "unexpected" [28].
  • Remove ads in-app purchase (one-time, priced for India, e.g. ₹99–₹199; validate pricing separately), visible in You → Settings and in a soft prompt after about the 5th interstitial. Never shown during a task.

6. Accessibility, one-handed use, low-end performance and the Indian market

6.1 Accessibility

  • Touch targets ≥ 48 × 48 dp; use Material IconButton or padding to reach that size [32].
  • TalkBack: live-region announcements in AR: "Width forty-five point five centimetres, plus or minus half a centimetre, good accuracy." Use specific click labels ("Add point" rather than "Double tap to activate") and custom actions on gallery cards (Place, Share, Delete) [32]. Test with Switch Access [32].
  • Non-visual and low-vision paths: manual dimension entry for everything; large-text support in AR overlays (scale with the system font up to 200%, and wrap the chip text); a high-contrast reticle option; never rely on colour alone (badges use shapes).
  • Reduce motion: respect the system animation setting; replace the looping coaching animation with static frames.
  • Sound cues (Android, because of the ARCore haptics caveat [4]) can be switched off; iOS uses haptics.
  • Colour-vision safety: the signal colour must pass for protanopia and deuteranopia; don't encode Good/Fair/Rough as green/amber/red alone.

  • Capability tiers decided at runtime (Depth API support varies by device [27]):

    • Tier B: ARCore without depth → plane-based measuring; card calibration strongly suggested.

  • Render at 30 fps when idle, and keep the UI overlay to Compose with minimal recomposition; avoid blur or glass effects over the camera feed (costly and "AI-looking").
  • Release the camera immediately when leaving AR; save in the background.
  • App size: keep the base APK small. Deliver heavy modules (e.g. a future photogrammetry mode) and Indic fonts beyond Devanagari as on-demand modules; the user may also need to install Google Play Services for AR, so show its download size ahead of time.
  • Thermal: after about 5 minutes of continuous AR, show a gentle "Phone getting warm? Take a break. Your progress is saved."
  • Offline-first: measuring, saving and the gallery work fully offline; sync and sharing links when online.

7. Visual direction: "precision instrument", not "AI app"

7.1 Principles

  • It reads like a tool, not a toy: think steel rule, vernier caliper, drafting sheet. Confident, quiet, exact.
  • Content first: the camera view is the hero; the UI is thin, sharp and minimal (Apple: "Let people use the entire display" [3]).
  • Show the working: dimension lines, extension lines, tick marks and tolerances are the decoration.

7.3 Light and dark

  • AR screens are always "dark chrome" (graphite pills over the camera) for legibility.
  • Non-AR screens follow the system theme: paper-white light theme (feels like a spec sheet) and a graphite dark theme. Keep contrast at WCAG AA or better for all text, including over the camera feed (ensured by solid pills).

8. Open questions for other research tracks

  1. Achievable accuracy by device tier: we need a bench test (10 target phones × 5 object types × 3 distances) to set the Good/Fair/Rough thresholds and the honest marketing copy.
  2. Card calibration feasibility on Tier B devices (ARCore without depth): expected improvement.
  3. 3D representation for partner catalogues: what format do retailers have (glTF, USDZ, only photos plus dimensions)? This decides whether "View in your room" shows a real model or a dimensioned card.
  4. Legal: DPDP consent for camera data, location tagging and ad IDs; partner co-branding rules; whether showing third-party ads next to retailer content is contractually allowed.

SpaceView: Business Potential and Monetization Strategy

Research date: 2 November 2026. Planning phase, no code yet. Prepared for a solo founder or small team in India.

How to read this. Figures marked [est.] are my own working assumptions, not sourced data. Most public AR-commerce statistics come from vendors and are inflated or poorly sourced. Where sources conflict, both are shown. USD to INR conversions use roughly ₹88/$.

0. Executive summary

  1. The opportunity is real, but "view in your room" alone is commoditised and under-used. Flipkart (via Scapic, 2021), Pepperfry (via Avataar), Samsung India and Lenskart already ship AR. Baymard's large-scale testing found only 6% of furniture shoppers proactively used "View in Room", and 87% of those who saw it chose not to use it. Measuring is also free on phones: Apple Measure and Samsung's built-in Quick Measure both exist. SpaceView's defensible angle is "measure your space once, then fit-check any product": a saved space profile that a retailer's product page can query ("✓ fits your TV wall"). Launching a full AR session every time is not the angle.
  2. Consumer ad revenue will be small. In India, banner eCPMs run about $0.10–0.50 and interstitials about $0.50–2.00. A realistic estimate is ~$25–150 (₹2k–13k) per month per 1,000 DAU, with a base case of about $55 (≈₹5k). "One interstitial per scan" is borderline under AdMob policy and harmful to retention. Cap it, and move most monetization to rewarded ads plus a cheap Pro tier.
  3. B2B is where the money is, but the big three are mostly locked up. Reliance Retail owns Fynd/GlamAR and Urban Ladder. Flipkart owns Scapic. Pepperfry works with Avataar. The best first targets are mid-tier and regional electronics chains, TV and appliance brands, D2C furniture brands, and Shopify-based D2C sellers.
  4. Best wedge: a parametric "TV and appliance fit check" for electronics. TVs, fridges, washing machines and ACs are near-boxes. 3D models can be generated from spec-sheet dimensions at almost no cost, with no scanning or artist time. Pitch it as a conversion and AOV (larger screen) uplift tool, not a returns reducer: Croma excludes size from returns, so wrong-size TVs are the customer's loss, not Croma's. Furniture is the returns-reduction story.
  5. The consumer and B2B editions must be separate builds. The B2B/white-label SDK and web embed must contain no AdMob SDK at all, because Google ads can show Amazon or Flipkart to a retailer's own shoppers.
  6. Recommended 12-month path: Android MVP with a fit-check gallery, then a Pro tier and a web embed, then 3–5 design-partner pilots, then paid pilots with a case study, then SaaS tiers. Year-1 B2B target: 3–6 paying customers, ₹1–3 lakh MRR [est.].

2.3 Is "one interstitial per scan" wise? No.

Policy:

  • AdMob's disallowed interstitial implementations prohibit interstitials on app load or exit, back-to-back interstitials, and ads that "surprise users" mid-task.
  • AdMob also says to place "no more than one interstitial ad after every two user actions" and only at logical breaks.
  • A scan is a multi-step task (calibrate, then tap points, then save). An ad that fires immediately after a scan, before the result is shown, is the kind of unexpected or recurring interstitial Google flags.

Banners:

  • AdMob says banners must not sit next to interactive buttons, float over content, or sit on screens with continuous interaction (AdMob guidance).
  • An AR camera view with a shutter or "place point" button is exactly that kind of screen. "Banners on all pages" must therefore exclude the AR/camera screens.

Retention: evidence is mostly vendor-sourced, but consistently negative.

  • One analysis put the D7 retention drop from heavy interstitials at 10–30%, unsourced (mwm.ai).
  • A published case found early interstitial exposure cut 14-day LTV by 7–11% (Airflux).
  • For a utility whose core action is the scan, penalising the core action is the worst place to put friction.

Recommended balanced cap:

  • No interstitials in the first session, or for the first 3 scans. This protects onboarding and the first "aha".
  • After that, at most 1 interstitial per 3 completed scans, and at most 1 per 3 minutes, ~4 per day. Show it only after the result is saved and displayed, at the transition back to the gallery, never between capture and result.
  • Banners only on the gallery, list and settings screens: adaptive anchored, not on the AR camera or the measurement-result overlay.
  • Rewarded ads as the main ad unit: "Watch an ad to unlock HD export, a PDF, or +5 gallery slots." This is user-initiated, so it carries no policy risk and earns better eCPM.
  • Use Firebase Remote Config and A/B testing to tune caps against D1/D7 retention (Firebase tutorial).
  • Pro users get no ads at all.

3. Conflict check: ads in a B2B / white-label product

Problem: AdMob serves whatever advertiser wins the auction. A Croma-branded AR experience could show an Amazon, Flipkart or Reliance Digital ad, and a Pepperfry embed could show a Wakefit ad. AdMob category and URL blocking reduces this but cannot guarantee it. Ad SDKs also collect device identifiers. Retailer InfoSec and DPDP reviews will flag any third-party ad SDK inside their app or site. The DPDP Rules were notified 13–14 Nov 2025, with main obligations phasing in through about May 2027 (Mondaq, Hogan Lovells).

4. B2B models

4.1 Product forms

  1. "Fit-check" web embed (lead product).
    • Built on Google <model-viewer> with ar-modes="webxr scene-viewer quick-look". That gives Android Chrome WebXR, Google Scene Viewer and iOS AR Quick Look from one GLB, with USDZ auto-generated for iOS (Khronos).
    • No app install is needed. This matters because most retail traffic is mobile web, and app-based AR adoption is low (Baymard).
    • Differentiator: alongside "View in your room", show "Does it fit? Enter or scan your space". A quick ARCore/ARKit or tape-entry measurement of the wall or alcove gives an instant ✓/✗ and a top-down overlay. This beats the low-usage pattern of AR-for-everything.
  2. White-label SDK (Android first, iOS later) for retailer apps: measure, scan, saved spaces, place. Higher price, longer sales cycle, InfoSec review. Offer it after the web embed proves value.
  3. Parametric 3D generation (core cost advantage).
    • TVs, monitors, fridges, washing machines, ACs, microwaves, mattresses and wardrobes are mostly boxes with known W×H×D, bezel and stand geometry.
    • Generate GLB/USDZ automatically from the retailer's spec feed (CSV, PIM or Shopify product JSON). Cost per SKU is about ₹0, so thousands of SKUs can go live in days.
    • This is the wedge against Avataar and GlamAR, which charge per modelled SKU.
  4. Per-SKU 3D model creation service for complex furniture (sofas, chairs, décor). Outsource to Indian studios such as Ikarus3D and mark up, or use photo-to-3D or GenAI tools with human QA. Do not build an art team.
  5. In-store "measure at home, buy in store" kiosk mode.
    • The customer saves their room profile in the consumer app or web.
    • Store staff at Croma, Vijay Sales or a regional chain pull it up by QR or phone number and show which models fit.
    • This bridges online research and the offline purchase, where most Indian TV sales still close.
  6. Analytics dashboard: AR and fit-check sessions, SKUs viewed, fit pass/fail, PDP→cart, and size intent ("users looking at 55" whose wall fits 65" → upsell list").

1. App size

1.1 Why it matters for India

  • Google's own Play data (2017, still the most-cited figure) shows each extra 6 MB lowers install conversion by about 1 %. In emerging markets, specifically India and Brazil, removing 10 MB raised conversion by about 2.5 % (Google Play blog, "Shrinking APKs, growing installs").
  • Play shows a non-blocking warning to mobile-data users when an app is larger than 200 MB. Current hard limits are: base module 500 MB, each feature module 500 MB, each asset pack 1.5 GB, 4 GB total for modules plus install-time packs, and 30 GB for on-demand/fast-follow packs (Play Console size limits). SpaceView will be nowhere near these limits, so the goal is conversion, not compliance.
  • AR adds a hidden cost. On phones without Google Play Services for AR (the ARCore runtime), the user also downloads roughly 36–38 MB (APKMirror listings for 1.47/1.48, unverified for current builds). With AR Required, Play installs it automatically. With AR Optional, the app must prompt for it (§6.5). The size of our own APK is the only part we control.

1.2 Where the bytes go (expected composition, arm64 split)

Component Rough download contribution Notes

play-services-ads (legacy) or GMA Next-Gen 1.5–3 MB (unverified, measure it) Google says Next-Gen is "smaller on device" than the full legacy SDK. The Lite SDK has been deprecated since Jan 2026 (Lite SDK page)

1.3 Build settings (aligned with Gridlark: AGP 9.4.1, Gradle 9.6.1, Kotlin 2.3.21, compileSdk 37, targetSdk 36)

  • Always upload an AAB. Play then serves per-ABI, per-density and per-language splits. Gridlark CI already builds :app:bundleRelease.
  • R8 full mode has been on by default since AGP 8.0. Never add android.enableR8.fullMode=false. AGP 9.0 made optimized resource shrinking the default when isShrinkResources = true, and AGP 9.1 repackages classes by default (Enable app optimization).
  • On AGP 9.3+ you can switch to the new DSL, which turns on code and resource optimisation and includes the default rules (equivalent to proguard-android-optimize.txt):
    android { buildTypes { release { optimization { enable = true } } } }
// keep rules go in src/main/keepRules/*.keep
    Gridlark's legacy form (isMinifyEnabled = true, isShrinkResources = true, proguard-android-optimize.txt) is still supported. Either is fine; pick one and use it in both projects.
  • ABIs: ship arm64-v8a and armeabi-v7a. Some 3 GB Indian phones still run 32-bit userspace, but ARCore certification of those phones is rare, so check the Play device catalogue once the app is live. Add x86_64 only in debug/benchmark builds for emulator and GMD tests. In an AAB, extra ABIs do not change what users download, but they do bloat the universal/test APKs.
    defaultConfig { ndk { abiFilters += listOf("arm64-v8a", "armeabi-v7a") } }
buildTypes { debug { ndk { abiFilters += "x86_64" } } }
  • Native libs uncompressed (useLegacyPackaging = false, the AGP default). This is required for 16 KB alignment and avoids an extracted duplicate on disk. The download stays compressed because Play compresses over the wire.
  • Resources: androidResources { localeFilters += listOf("en", "hi") }. Add more Indian languages later through Play language splits. Strip unused com.google.android.gms locales this way too.
  • Exclude META-INF/*.kotlin_module, *.version, DebugProbesKt.bin and similar in packaging.resources.excludes. Gridlark already excludes AL2.0/LGPL2.1.
  • Do not add mediation in v1. Each adapter brings a full third-party ad SDK (often 2–8 MB each, unverified, so measure before adding), plus more Data-safety rows and more ANR surface.

1.4 Play Feature Delivery / on-demand modules: use sparingly

  • On-demand modules need SplitCompat, network at first use, a UI for "downloading…", and testing with bundletool --local-testing. Feature modules cannot enable minify themselves (the base module controls R8). Exported activities in feature modules are not allowed (Play Feature Delivery).
  • AR is SpaceView's core feature. Putting ARCore and Filament on-demand would make every new user wait through a second download before the first measurement, which is a worse UX than 3–5 MB extra at install. Recommendation: AR/3D rendering stays in the base module in v1.
  • Candidates for on-demand delivery later:
    • an ML segmentation/edge-refinement model (e.g. 3–10 MB TFLite/LiteRT) for better scan cutouts. SceneView's own release notes mention a ~10 MB on-device detector that is "not in the APK" and is downloaded on first use (SceneView releases);
    • a furniture/sample model catalogue.
  • Conditional delivery (<dist:device-feature dist:name="android.hardware.camera.ar"/>) only makes sense if we choose AR Optional and also want the 3D viewer without Filament. We don't, because the gallery's 3D preview needs Filament too.

1.5 Play Asset Delivery (PAD) for 3D assets

  • PAD works for apps as well as games. On-demand packs do not count toward the listed app size; install-time packs do (Play Asset Delivery). Use fast-follow for a small "starter furniture" pack and on-demand for larger catalogues. Assets in packs are read through AssetPackManager file paths, so no extra copy is made.
  • v1 has no catalogue (the gallery is user-generated), so PAD is not needed for v1. Plan for it once more than ~2 MB of bundled models exists.

2. Startup and runtime performance

2.1 Baseline and Startup Profiles without Android Studio

  • Baseline Profiles precompile the hot paths. Startup Profiles also reorder DEX so startup code sits in classes.dex, which is "usually 15–30 % faster than Baseline Profiles alone". They need R8 enabled and AGP ≥ 8.3 for DEX layout optimisation (on by default) (Startup Profiles).
  • Generate them entirely from Gradle with a Gradle Managed Device. The image must be AOSP because the generator needs root (Configure Baseline Profile generation):
    // :baselineprofile (com.android.test + androidx.baselineprofile plugin)
android {
  testOptions.managedDevices.allDevices {
    create<com.android.build.api.dsl.ManagedVirtualDevice>("pixel6Api34") {
      device = "Pixel 6"; apiLevel = 34; systemImageSource = "aosp"
    }
  }
}
baselineProfile { managedDevices += "pixel6Api34"; useConnectedDevices = false }
// :app
baselineProfile { saveInSrc = true; mergeIntoMain = true }   // commit the generated profile
    Run ./gradlew :app:generateBaselineProfile (or generateReleaseBaselineProfile). Output goes to src/main/generated/baselineProfiles/{baseline-prof.txt,startup-prof.txt}. In the generator, call rule.collect(packageName, includeInStartupProfile = true) { … } for the launch journey.
  • CI plan: a separate GitHub Actions workflow (workflow_dispatch plus weekly cron, or on v* tags) on ubuntu-latest with KVM enabled through the usual udev rule. GitHub's Linux runners support hardware-accelerated emulators (android-emulator-runner README). The workflow regenerates profiles and opens a PR with the diff. Do not use automaticGenerationDuringBuild = true in the normal build, because it doubles build time.
  • Journeys to profile: cold launch → home; home → gallery grid scroll; gallery → 3D viewer. AR camera screens cannot run on an emulator (an ARCore emulator needs a special virtual scene), so profile up to "AR screen composed", not tracking itself.
  • The compiled profile must be under 1.5 MB. Keep journeys focused.
  • Gridlark already depends on androidx.profileinstaller. SpaceView should do the same, so sideloaded/internal builds also get the profile.

2.2 Startup architecture

  • Application.onCreate should be close to empty. Lazy-initialise everything: Room DB on first use, AdMob after consent on a background thread (§5), ARCore availability check deferred (below), Filament Engine created only on entering a 3D/AR screen.
  • Use the App Startup library instead of content providers for any SDK that insists on auto-init. Remove auto-init providers we don't need with tools:node="remove" (e.g. WorkManager initialiser if unused) (Launch time guide).
  • Use the SplashScreen API (core-splashscreen, as in Gridlark). No custom splash activity.
  • ARCore availability: Google recommends calling ArCoreApk.checkAvailabilityAsync() early and ignoring the result so a cached answer is ready when the AR button is shown (Enable ARCore). Do this after the first frame (Choreographer/LaunchedEffect on the home screen), not in Application.
  • Report TTFD with ReportDrawnWhen { galleryLoaded } so Macrobenchmark and vitals measure the real "usable" time.
  • Targets: cold TTID ≤ 800 ms on mid-range, ≤ 1.5 s on 3 GB phones. Vitals thresholds are cold ≥ 5 s, warm ≥ 2 s and hot ≥ 1.5 s (vitals launch time).

2.3 Compose performance

  • Strong skipping is on by default since Kotlin 2.0.20, so we're covered. Unstable parameters can still skip by instance equality, and lambdas are auto-remembered (Strong skipping). We still want:
    • @Immutable/@Stable on UI models, or kotlinx.collections.immutable for lists;
    • a stabilityConfigurationFiles entry for domain types from non-Compose modules (e.g. java.time.*, our Dimensions value class);
    • Compose compiler reports behind a Gradle property, checked occasionally.
  • Lazy grids for the gallery: key = { it.id } and contentType. Thumbnails load through Coil 3 with explicit size(), which avoids decoding full-size images.
  • Defer state reads: use lambda modifiers (Modifier.offset { }, graphicsLayer { }, drawBehind) for anything that animates per frame.
  • The AR overlay is the main jank risk. Do not push per-frame AR data (pose, distance, tracking state) into Compose state at 30–60 Hz:
    • draw reticles and measurement lines in the 3D renderer, not in Compose;
    • expose a StateFlow throttled to about 10 Hz with distinctUntilChanged() (e.g. distance rounded to 1 mm) for labels, collected with collectAsStateWithLifecycle();
    • use snapshotFlow/conflate() for any reverse direction.
  • Always benchmark release-like builds (benchmarkRelease). Debug Compose is much slower.

2.4 Threading and the AR frame loop

  • ARCore's session.update() and rendering run on the GL/render thread (SceneView manages this). That thread must never do disk or network I/O, Bitmap compression, Room queries or JSON parsing.
  • Do heavy work off the render and main threads:
    • YUV→RGB conversion, texture atlas building, JPEG/WebP encode, GLB writing: use Dispatchers.Default.limitedParallelism(2). Limiting parallelism stops scan processing from starving ARCore's VIO tracking threads. ARCore logs "VIO frequency low" when CPU starvation hurts tracking (ARCore performance);
    • Room and file I/O: use Dispatchers.IO.
  • CPU camera images: the default CPU stream is 640×480 and costs nothing extra. Any higher resolution adds a second stream, which costs real CPU/memory (ARCore ML guide). For photo textures, capture one higher-resolution frame only when the user presses "Capture". Don't stream it.
  • Always close camera images: frame.acquireCameraImage().use { … }. Treat NotYetAvailableException as "try the next frame". Holding images causes ResourceExhaustedException. Copy the planes out quickly and process the copy on a worker thread.
  • Use CameraConfigFilter.setTargetFps(EnumSet.of(TARGET_FPS_30)) and setDepthSensorUsage(DO_NOT_USE) unless depth is needed. 60 fps "increases power consumption and memory usage and is not supported on all devices", and 30 fps is available on every ARCore device (CameraConfig.TargetFps).

2.5 GPU and rendering budget (renderer-agnostic, Filament-centric)

  • Render at camera rate (30 fps). Don't render extra frames when nothing changes in the non-AR 3D viewer: render on demand, or pause the Choreographer callback when idle.
  • Low tier (≤ 4 GB RAM or Mali-G52-class GPU): no SSAO, no bloom, FXAA or no AA (no MSAA), one directional light with a ≤ 1024 shadow map (or a fake contact-shadow blob), dynamic resolution allowed down to 0.75.
  • Light estimation: AMBIENT_INTENSITY on the low tier; ENVIRONMENTAL_HDR only on the high tier, because HDR cubemap estimation costs CPU/GPU.
  • Plane finding: HORIZONTAL (add vertical only for wall measuring). Disable plane finding once an object is placed. Disable Instant Placement once full tracking exists. ARCore lists both as CPU-heavy (ARCore performance).
  • Depth API (over 88 % of active devices support it, per Google as of May 2026): useful for measurement on non-planar surfaces and for occlusion, but expensive. Make it opt-in or automatic only on the high tier, and turn it off under thermal pressure.
  • Choose the tier with ActivityManager.isLowRamDevice(), MemoryInfo.totalMem, memoryClass and the GPU renderer string, all cached in DataStore on first launch.

2.6 Memory on low-RAM phones

  • Vitals now has memory core vitals: Anon RSS + swap P99 per RAM tier (4 GB tier, foreground: 2.0 GB) and bitmap memory (200 MB). Play may reduce visibility for apps over these thresholds from February 2027 (Android vitals; memory usage). Phones below 4 GB have no threshold, but they are where low-memory kills happen. Watch the LMK vital.
  • Budget for an AR session on a 3–4 GB phone: ≤ 500–700 MB. The main consumers:
    • camera buffers (owned by ARCore);
    • Filament: an engine with ~5–20 MB of buffers plus textures. Each 1024² texture with mips ≈ 5.3 MB of GPU memory; a 2048² one ≈ 21 MB. Cap user textures at 1024² on the low tier and limit simultaneous placed objects (e.g. 6 on the low tier);
    • an AdMob banner WebView (tens of MB; destroy, not just hide, banners before entering AR).
  • Respond to onTrimMemory(TRIM_MEMORY_UI_HIDDEN / BACKGROUND): release the Filament scene and textures and clear the Coil memory cache.
  • Filament objects are native and not garbage-collected. Every Texture, MaterialInstance, Entity, IndirectLight and Engine must be destroyed explicitly. LeakCanary won't see these leaks, so check with adb shell dumpsys meminfo <pkg> before and after 10 cycles of entering and leaving the 3D screen.
  • ARCore session lifecycle: create once per AR screen; pause() in onPause (frees the camera); close() when the screen is destroyed. Avoid fast create/destroy loops; older ARCore versions leaked memory on repeated session creation (EasyAR notes for ARCore 1.13, unverified for current versions).
  • Don't retain Activity, View or Session in ViewModels. Hold AR state in a screen-scoped holder that's disposed through DisposableEffect/lifecycle observers.

Do: GMD-generated Baseline and Startup profiles; empty Application.onCreate; throttled AR→Compose state; 30 fps, depth off, plane finding off after placement; device tiering; explicit Filament destruction; close every Image.

3. Storage design (AR Gallery)

3.1 Layout (app-private, so no storage permissions)

filesDir/gallery/<uuid>/model.glb      // box/low-poly mesh + 1024² JPEG atlas, 150–500 KB
filesDir/gallery/<uuid>/thumb.webp     // 384 px, ~15–25 KB
filesDir/gallery/<uuid>/source.jpg     // OPTIONAL original capture, off by default (saves space)
cacheDir/capture/…                      // temp frames, deleted after the save completes
  • Room (as in Gridlark: Room 2.8.x + KSP, exported schemas) holds metadata: id, name, width/height/depth in mm (store integers in millimetres, never floats in cm), created/modified, file sizes, thumbnail path, schema version, measurement accuracy/confidence.
  • DataStore (Preferences) holds settings: units, device tier cache, consent-derived flags, ad frequency counters.
  • Writes are atomic. Write to model.glb.tmp, fsync, then rename, then insert the Room row in a transaction. On startup, a sweep deletes orphan directories and rows whose files are missing.
  • Scoped storage: no READ_MEDIA_IMAGES or WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE. Play's Photo & Video policy allows broad media permissions only for apps whose core purpose is broad media access. Everyone else must use the Photo Picker (Photo & Video Permissions policy). To import a photo, use PickVisualMedia. To export, use MediaStore inserts into Pictures/SpaceView (no permission needed on API 29+) and FileProvider + share sheet for .glb.
  • Before saving: check StorageManager.getAllocatableBytes() and show a clear "storage full" message. Many Indian low-end phones run close to full.
  • Cache eviction: cacheDir/capture is cleared after every scan and on launch; Coil disk cache ≤ 50 MB; never put gallery data in cacheDir, because the OS may delete it.
  • Backup: Auto Backup is capped at 25 MB per app. Either exclude gallery/ and the Room DB together in dataExtractionRules (so no rows point at missing files), or include both and accept partial restore. Recommended for v1: exclude both, and offer an explicit "Export all" (.zip) later.
  • Image pipeline: capture frame → crop/rectify each face on a worker thread → downscale with Bitmap.createScaledBitmap or inSampleSize to ≤ 1024 px per face/atlas → recycle intermediates → JPEG q80 into the GLB. Display thumbnails with Coil (size(256)). Use Bitmap.Config.HARDWARE for read-only display bitmaps to keep them out of the Java heap.
  • Approximate storage per scan: 0.2–0.5 MB, so 100 scans ≈ 20–50 MB. Show total gallery size in Settings.

Do: private storage, Room in millimetres, atomic writes, no media permissions, a consistent backup policy, an allocatable-bytes check.

4. Battery and thermal behaviour in long AR sessions

  • AR keeps the camera, VIO/SLAM, GPU and display busy all at once. On low-end SoCs, a session can reach THERMAL_STATUS_MODERATE within minutes.
  • Monitoring (ARCore performance; ADPF thermal):
    • PowerManager.addThermalStatusListener (API 29+) and getThermalHeadroom(forecastSeconds) (API 30+). Call headroom at most once every 10 s (more often returns NaN). A NaN or 0 first value means the API isn't supported.
    • Step-down ladder: headroom ≥ 0.85 or MODERATE → disable Depth and HDR light estimation, dynamic resolution to 0.75, shadows off. Headroom ≥ 0.95 or SEVERE → pause the AR session and show "Phone is warm, AR paused". CRITICAL → end the session.
  • Idle auto-pause: if there's no touch and the reticle hasn't moved for about 60 s, pause the session and show "Tap to resume". That saves battery, and the camera LED/indicator turns off.
  • Use FLAG_KEEP_SCREEN_ON on the AR window only. Never use partial wake locks. "Excessive partial wake locks" is a core vital with a 5 % threshold (Android vitals).
  • No background work during or after AR. No WorkManager jobs are needed in v1.
  • Set the display refresh rate preference for the AR screen to 60 Hz (preferredDisplayModeId/Surface.setFrameRate(30f, …) on the render surface). This stops 120 Hz panels burning power on a 30 fps camera feed.
  • Test protocol: a 15-minute continuous session on a 4 GB MediaTek Helio G-series phone and a Snapdragon 6-series phone. Log thermal status, fps, battery % and dropped frames, and record a Perfetto trace. Google's ADPF guidance recommends sessions of at least 15 minutes to see thermal stabilisation.

5. AdMob performance and compliance

5.1 SDK choice

  • Legacy play-services-ads 25.x (Gridlark uses 25.5.0 + UMP 4.0.0): mature and supports all mediation. Since 24.0.0, OPTIMIZE_INITIALIZATION and OPTIMIZE_AD_LOADING are on by default (Optimize initialization).
  • GMA Next-Gen SDK (com.google.android.libraries.ads.mobile.sdk:ads-mobile-sdk, 1.6.0 on the quick-start page):
    • minSdk 24;
    • smaller on device than the legacy SDK (per Google);
    • built-in preloading API: call start once, then pollAd(); the SDK refills and retries automatically; at most 6 preloaded ads in total; a buffer of 2 per preload ID is recommended (Next-Gen ad preloading);
    • callbacks arrive on a background thread, so UI work must be posted to the main thread;
    • mediation only works through AdMob mediation.
    • Third-party posts say it reached 1.x in April 2026 and became Google's "preferred" Android SDK in July 2026 (unverified).
  • Recommendation: choose Next-Gen for SpaceView if the official page shows it as GA when implementation starts. We have no mediation in v1, size matters, and preloading fits the "interstitial after scan" pattern. Otherwise use legacy 25.x to match Gridlark. Either way, keep the ads behind a small AdsGateway interface so they can be swapped.

5.2 Initialisation order (no main-thread blocking)

  1. First frame drawn (home screen).
  2. Call UMP requestConsentInfoUpdate on every launch. Show the form if needed. canRequestAds() returns false until the update has been called (UMP privacy).
  3. When canRequestAds() is true, call MobileAds.initialize() on a background thread (Dispatchers.IO), once per process. The Next-Gen docs warn that initialising on the main thread may cause an ANR (Next-Gen quick start).
  4. If there's no mediation, MobileAds.disableMediationAdapterInitialization() (legacy) skips adapter start-up work.
  5. Start preloading the interstitial when the user enters the scan flow, not at app start. That saves data and memory on users who only browse.

5.3 Placement rules (performance + policy)

  • No ads on the AR camera screen, neither banner nor interstitial, during tracking:
    • a banner WebView costs memory and GPU composition on top of the camera surface;
    • it invites accidental clicks;
    • it competes with VIO for CPU. Destroy the banner AdView when navigating into AR and recreate it afterwards.
  • Interstitial "per scan" is shown at the transition after "Saved to gallery", never in the middle of an AR session and never on app open/exit. AdMob explicitly disallows interstitials on app load or exit, after every user action, immediately after another interstitial, or ones that launch unexpectedly (AdMob interstitial guidance). A completed scan is a reasonable natural break.
    • Add a frequency cap anyway: first scan of the session ad-free, then at most one per 3 minutes and at most one per 2 scans. This protects ratings and AdMob account health.
  • Only show a preloaded ad. If none is ready, skip it. Never show a spinner while waiting for an ad.
  • Adaptive anchored banners go only on gallery and list screens. Pause them in onPause.
  • Mute video ads (MobileAds.setAppMuted(true) / Next-Gen equivalent) to save bandwidth and avoid annoyance.
  • Data use: each banner refresh costs mobile data, so use the default 60 s refresh or longer, not 30 s.

5.4 Consent and privacy for India

  • UMP messages are legally required for the EEA/UK/Switzerland (GDPR) and US state-privacy regimes. India is not a UMP-message region. Still ship UMP: it's already in Gridlark, Play distribution may extend beyond India, and it provides the privacy-options entry point.
  • DPDP Act 2023 / DPDP Rules 2025:
    • the Rules were notified on 14 Nov 2025 with phased commencement;
    • notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, data principal rights and children's data rules apply 18 months later (~13–14 May 2027; law-firm sources differ by a day) (PIB/MeitY doc; The Week).
    • Implications for planning:
      1. Keep all scan images and measurements on the device (not collected). This makes SpaceView a very small data fiduciary.
      2. Publish a clear privacy notice in English and Hindi before launch.
      3. The DPDP Act treats anyone under 18 as a child and bars tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children. Set the Play target audience to 18+ (or 13+ without child-directed content), and plan for non-personalised ads (npa/Limited Ads, or a "tag for under age of consent") if child users are possible. Legal review needed before May 2027.
  • Advertising ID: play-services-ads merges com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID. Complete the Play Console Advertising ID declaration (required for target 33+).

Do: Next-Gen (if GA) or legacy 25.x; init after consent, off the main thread; preload at scan-flow entry; no ads in AR; frequency cap; no mediation in v1; DPDP-ready privacy notice by May 2027.

6. Play Store requirements (as of November 2026)

6.1 Target API level

  • New apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36) from 31 Aug 2026. Existing apps must target ≥ 35 to stay visible to new users on newer Android versions (Target API requirements). Expect API 37 to be required around Aug 2027. compileSdk 37 is already in Gridlark and is required by SceneView 4.65.
  • Behaviour changes to plan for at target 36:
    • Edge-to-edge is enforced (since target 35). Use Compose WindowInsets and place AR controls in safe areas.
    • Orientation and resizability: on large screens (smallest width ≥ 600 dp), target 36 ignores screenOrientation, resizeableActivity and min/max aspect ratio. An opt-out exists at API 36 only; there is no opt-out at API 37 (Android 16 orientation changes).
      • The AR screen must therefore handle rotation and resizing on tablets and foldables. Update ARCore display geometry on configuration change and avoid recreating the session on rotation (configChanges with a carefully handled onConfigurationChanged, or a ViewModel-held session).
      • Phones under 600 dp can still lock portrait.
    • Predictive back is enabled by default for target 36. Make sure the AR screen's back handling (e.g. "discard measurement?") uses PredictiveBackHandler/BackHandler.

6.2 16 KB memory page size (critical for ARCore + Filament)

  • Rule: apps targeting Android 15+ must support 16 KB pages on 64-bit devices. From 1 Feb 2027, Play blocks updates that don't (16 KB page sizes guide). Without it, the app fails or runs in "page size compat mode" with a warning dialog on 16 KB devices (Pixel 8+ dev option, newer OEM phones).
  • Requirements:
    • AGP ≥ 8.5.1 (we have 9.4) with uncompressed native libs, which zip-aligns them to 16 KB;
    • every .so with LOAD segments aligned to 2**14 and RELRO correct;
    • NDK r28+ for anything we compile ourselves (16 KB by default).
  • ARCore: Unity's ARCore plugin changelog says ARCore 1.48 fixed 16 KB alignment for its binaries (inferred, unverified from Google's own notes). Current is 1.57.0 (16 Oct 2026), which targets API 37 (ARCore SDK releases). Use the latest.
  • Filament / SceneView: Filament's release notes don't mention 16 KB, but the flutter_sceneview 4.24 changelog lists "Google Play 16 KB page-size alignment for Filament native libs" fixes (flutter_sceneview changelog). The latest is SceneView 4.65.0 (1 Nov 2026), with Filament 1.79.0 (26 Oct 2026). Verify in CI. Don't trust the changelogs alone.
  • CI check (Linux runner, no Studio needed):
    $ANDROID_HOME/build-tools/36.0.0/zipalign -c -P 16 -v 4 app-release.apk   # "Verification successful"
# plus, for every lib/arm64-v8a/*.so and lib/x86_64/*.so:
llvm-objdump -p lib.so | grep LOAD        # every 'align 2**14' (or higher)
# or simply: check_elf_alignment.sh app-release.apk  (AOSP script) -> all ALIGNED
bundletool dump config --bundle=app.aab | grep -i alignment   # expect PAGE_ALIGNMENT_16K
    Also run the app on a 16 KB emulator image in the GMD job (Google APIs Experimental 16 KB Page Size x86_64 system image).

Recommendation: AR Required for v1.

  • Even with AR Required, still call checkAvailability and requestInstall before creating the session, because users can sideload or have an outdated ARCore. Handle UnavailableUserDeclinedInstallationException cleanly.

6.6 Android vitals (what Play enforces)

  • Core vitals:
    • user-perceived crash rate: 1.09 % overall, 8 % per phone model;
    • user-perceived ANR rate: 0.47 % overall, 8 % per phone model;
    • excessive partial wake locks: 5 %;
    • memory: Anon RSS + swap P99 per RAM tier, and bitmap memory. Memory and DEX code optimisation thresholds may affect visibility from Feb 2027. The DEX vital applies only to apps with > 10 MB DEX, which we won't reach, but enabling R8 covers it anyway (Android vitals; DEX code optimisation).
  • Play uses a 28-day rolling window. Set up email alerts and use the Play Developer Reporting API if we automate dashboards.
  • AR-specific crash and ANR risks:
    • GPU driver crashes in Filament on specific Mali/PowerVR devices: watch per-device rates and exclude broken models in the device catalogue if needed;
    • ANRs from synchronous ARCore install checks or session creation on the main thread: create the session off the main thread where the API allows, and never block the UI on checkAvailability;
    • the main thread stalling on GLB writes.

7. Quality tooling

7.1 R8 keep rules

  • First rely on consumer rules shipped in the AARs: ARCore, play-services-ads, Room, Compose and Coil all ship them. Add our own rules only after a release-build crash names the missing class.
  • Filament/gltfio/SceneView:
    • JNI entry points are kept by Filament's native-method rules; check whether the SceneView AAR ships consumer-rules.pro (unverified; ask the rendering agent to check).
    • If R8 breaks JNI callbacks, keep only the classes/methods called from native code (e.g. -keepclasseswithmembernames class * { native <methods>; } is already in the default rules).
    • Avoid -keep class com.google.android.filament.** { *; }. Broad package keeps stop most optimisation, and they show up in the R8 Configuration Analyzer (R8 keep rules blog).
  • kotlinx.serialization (if used for metadata) needs the official rules, which the plugin now ships.
  • Keep the mapping.txt upload in CI (Gridlark already archives it). Upload it to Play automatically with the release, so vitals stack traces are deobfuscated. Upload native debug symbols where available.
  • Use the R8 Configuration Analyzer (AGP ≥ 9.3) and inspect BUNDLE-METADATA/com.android.tools/r8.json from the AAB to track the optimisation, obfuscation and shrinking percentages Play sees (DEX code optimisation).
  • Test the release build on a real device every sprint. R8 bugs only appear in release builds.

7.2 LeakCanary and StrictMode (debug only)

  • LeakCanary: debugImplementation("com.squareup.leakcanary:leakcanary-android:2.14") (stable). 3.0 is still alpha (3.0-alpha-9), so don't use it. LeakCanary catches Activity, Fragment, ViewModel and View leaks. It won't catch Filament or ARCore native leaks (see §2.6 meminfo protocol).
  • StrictMode in debug Application: ThreadPolicy.detectDiskReads/Writes/Network().penaltyLog() and VmPolicy.detectLeakedClosableObjects().detectActivityLeaks().detectLeakedRegistrationObjects().detectUnsafeIntentLaunch(). Expect some noise from the AdMob and ARCore SDKs; allowlist them by stack.
  • debugImplementation only. Nothing debug-only may reach release (lint and the dependency check enforce this).

    • well-tuned Compose apps with Baseline and Startup profiles reach about 400–700 ms TTID on mid-range phones (industry rule of thumb, not an official figure);
    • Startup Profiles are worth a further 15–30 % (official);
    • Play's floor is 5 s cold, 2 s warm and 1.5 s hot.
    • Our targets: ≤ 800 ms mid-range, ≤ 1.5 s on 3 GB phones.
  • Stability: top apps sit around 0.1–0.3 % crash rate and below 0.1–0.2 % ANR rate, versus Play's bad-behaviour lines of 1.09 % and 0.47 %. Our targets: crash < 0.3 % and ANR < 0.15 % overall, and no single phone model above 2 %.

The maths is mostly correct and carefully written. 1. engine/src/main correctness

MAJOR-1: The public distance and rectangle APIs give about 32% coverage, not 95%, unless the caller remembers to add endpoint-localisation error

Uncertainty.kt:189 (UncertaintyModel.distance), Uncertainty.kt:164-186, PointEstimator.kt:184, Shapes.kt:66

PointEstimate.point.covariance is the standard error of the mean of per-frame jitter. The user's aiming or tap error and the hit-test bias stay the same for the whole 0.5–1 s hold, so averaging cannot see them. distance(), rectangle() and Calibrator take that covariance as the whole random error. The only thing that adds the localisation term is an optional withExtraSigma(), and the only test that proves 95% coverage (displayedBoundCoversTruth) calls it by hand.

Failing scenario (probe). Endpoints have a 3 mm per-axis aim error (about 1.5 px at 1 m on VGA) plus 1 mm frame jitter, 30 samples, NO_DEPTH tier, fed through PointEstimator → model.distance():

True length Mean shown ±95 Actual coverage
8.56 cm (card) ±1.8 mm 32%
30 cm ±6.0 mm 75%
1 m ±2.0 cm 92% (scale term dominates)

The same flaw feeds calibration. A card or A4 measured this way reports a factor σ that is far too small. The TOO_NOISY gate (Calibration.kt:148) then never trips, and the calibrated ± falls to the 0.3% floor, which the user sees as a "precise" ±0.6%.

Fix. Make the safe path the only path:

  • Have distance, height, rectangle and boxFromPoints take an endpoint type that carries the localisation inputs (range, focal px and pixel error, or an explicit σ), for example EndpointObservation(estimate, rangeM, focalPx, pixelSigma).
  • Alternatively, give UncertaintyModel a required minEndpointSigma(range) that is always added in quadrature.
  • Also consider the research's absolute floor ("never better than ±1 cm" for non-LiDAR Android) as a tier-level absFloor95.
  • Add a test that runs the scenario above and requires coverage ≥ 0.9. It fails today.

MAJOR-2: The autocorrelation correction under-covers on correlated AR samples (55–82% instead of 95%)

PointEstimator.kt:179-184, PointEstimator.kt:205-206 (MAX_RHO = 0.9)

Consecutive AR frames are strongly correlated: hand tremor and slow VIO wobble are low-frequency. Probe: AR(1) jitter with σ = 3 mm, 30 samples, checking the true point against ±1.96·SE along x:

ρ Coverage Mean n_eff
0.5 0.90 12.5
0.8 0.82 5.9
0.9 0.70 4.3
0.95 0.55 3.6

There are four causes:

  1. The lag-1 ρ estimate from 30 samples is biased low (about −(1+4ρ)/n).
  2. ρ is capped at 0.9.
  3. With strong correlation, the sample variance itself underestimates the process variance.
  4. k = 1.96 is used even when n_eff is about 3–6 (Student-t would be about 2.6–4.3).

Existing tests miss this. standardErrorIsCalibrated turns the correction off and uses i.i.d. data. autocorrelationInflatesStandardError only checks that the SE grows.

Fix. Any of these closes most of the gap:

  • Use the bias-corrected ρ: ρ̂ + (1+4ρ̂)/n.
  • Raise the cap to about 0.97.
  • Apply the variance correction to sampleCov as well: multiply by 1/(1 − 2ρ/(n(1−ρ))).
  • Use a Student-t factor for n_eff < 30. Simplest of all, floor nEff at a physically motivated maximum, for example "≤ 1 independent sample per 100 ms of hold".

Add an AR(1) coverage test at ρ = 0.9 that requires ≥ 0.9.

MAJOR-3: The rectangle badge ignores geometric consistency, so a wrong number can be shown as FAIR or GOOD

Shapes.kt:39, Shapes.kt:66-116

RectangleMeasurement.quality is worst(width, height). Neither of those reflects corner order, squareness or planarity. The research (§2.6) says the angle deviation must feed the uncertainty. Probes:

  • Bow-tie (Z) tap order. Corners TL, TR, BL, BR on a 60 × 40 cm frame give H = 72.1 cm ± 1.5 (true 40 cm), badge FAIR, maxRightAngleDeviationDeg = 56°. Both "height" edges are diagonals and agree with each other, so the half-difference σ guard does nothing.
  • Corners 4 cm off the supplied plane (bad depth or wrong plane), DEPTH_SENSOR tier: planarityRms = 0.04, W = 60 cm ± 0.5, badge GOOD.

Fix.

  • Reorder the snapped corners by angle around their centroid in the plane before computing edges, or reject non-convex and self-intersecting quads, as Homography.rectify already does.
  • Fold maxRightAngleDeviationDeg, diagonalMismatch and planarityRms into the width/height σ, for example σ_geom = ½·|diag mismatch| and planarity × tan(view angle).
  • Force ROUGH when !isSquare or when planarityRms is above about 1 cm.

MAJOR-4: Plane fitting accepts near-collinear (strip) point sets and returns a confident, tilted plane

Plane.kt:260, Plane.kt:145

The degeneracy test only rejects λ₁ ≤ 1e-12·λ₂, which means exactly collinear points. Probe: 200 depth or feature points in a 1 m × 2 cm strip on a wall (a skirting line, a narrow textured band on a plain wall, the edge of a frame), with 3 mm noise. RANSAC returns a fit 200/200 times, with normal tilt up to 8.4°, a small rmsResidual and a tiny offsetStandardError.

At 30 cm from the strip, 8.4° puts the plane about 4.4 cm off. Every ray–plane corner beyond the strip inherits that error, yet the ± contains no term for it. Plain painted walls are the research's top risk (§6.4-1), and these inputs are exactly what such walls produce.

Fix.

  • Reject or flag fits where √λ₁ (in-plane minor extent) is less than about 10 × rmsResidual, or λ₁/λ₀ < about 25.
  • Expose a normal-angle σ ≈ rms / (√λ₁ · √n) in PlaneFit, and propagate it as a lateral term proportional to the distance from the centroid.
  • Add a test: a strip fit must be null or flagged.

MINOR-1: PointEstimator.estimate() can return NaN, and the next engine call crashes

PointEstimator.kt:159-160

The comment "m ≥ 1 always" is false. Being within one MAD holds per axis, not jointly. Probe: with 15 samples, give each axis a different majority subset of exact-equal values so the three subsets have no common member. The result is inlierCount = 0 and a NaN position. model.distance() then throws IllegalArgumentException from RawLength.init, which would happen on the AR frame thread. It is rare, because it needs strongly clustered or quantised samples, but the fix is trivial.

Fix. if (m == 0) → fall back to the per-axis medians with SE = 1.2533·MAD/√n, or return null and mark the estimate unstable.

MINOR-2: planeBiasRel divides by the wrong distance

Uncertainty.kt:259-262

A plane offset δ scales ray-intersected lengths by δ/h, where h is the perpendicular camera-to-plane distance, not the viewing range. If a caller passes the range h/cosθ, the term is too small by cosθ: 0.71 at 45°.

Fix. Take cameraPosition: Vec3 and compute abs(fit.plane.signedDistance(cam)). Also consider adding the plane term inside rectangle() automatically when a PlaneFit is supplied.

MINOR-3: The rectangle snaps corners by orthogonal projection, not ray–plane intersection

Shapes.kt:73-78

A corner off the plane by δ along the viewing ray keeps a lateral error of δ·sinθ after orthogonal projection. Ray intersection, as the research (§2.3 and §6.2) prescribes, removes it. The covariance shown is also wrong, because projection drops the normal component without adding the lateral leak.

Fix. Document that M2 must pass ray-intersected corners (Plane.intersect), or add a rectangleFromRays(rays, planeFit) overload.

MINOR-4: A calibration can be accepted even when it makes the ± wider

Calibration.kt:84, Uncertainty.kt:146-150

MAX_REL_SIGMA = 1% is looser than the NO_DEPTH prior σ (2%/1.96 = 1.02%). Probe: a calibration with relSigma 0.99% is accepted. With the floor the effective σ becomes 1.034%, which is wider than the uncalibrated 1.020%, while the app tells the user they are in "precision mode".

Fix. Accept only when √(relSigma² + floor²) < about 0.7 × the tier prior.

MINOR-5: The calibration factor band and scope differ from the research

Calibration.kt:80-81

  • The research says to apply s only when 0.9 < s < 1.1. The code allows 0.85–1.15. A 12–15% scale error means tracking is broken, so refuse it.
  • UncertaintyModel.withCalibration applies the factor to every measured length in the session. The research says the factor corrects scale "for that plane and session". Depth bias is distance- and plane-dependent (δZ/Z).

Fix. Store the plane and range in ScaleCalibration. Widen the floor or skip the factor for other planes, or for ranges outside about ±50% of the calibration range.

MINOR-6: A box with an unmeasured preset depth and σ = 0 is badged GOOD

Shapes.kt:50, Uncertainty.kt:44

A wall-hung frame with the 2 cm "thin frame" preset passed as RawLength(0.02, 0.0, isMeasured = false) gives a GOOD box. The research (§3.3) says D must be labelled "low confidence" or "estimate".

Fix. Exclude unmeasured dimensions from BoxMeasurement.quality, and expose an explicit isEstimate flag per dimension for the UI.

MINOR-7: FitPolicy.allowRotation also lets the item be tipped onto its side

FitVerdict.kt:73-74

All 3! permutations are tried, so a 180 cm fridge can "fit" a 70 cm-high niche lying down. The test rotationFindsBestOrientation encodes exactly this for 2D (W↔H).

Fix. Add rotation = NONE | ABOUT_VERTICAL | ANY, with ABOUT_VERTICAL as the default. It permutes only the horizontal axes, W and D.

MINOR-8: Homography edge cases

Homography.kt:85-90, Homography.kt:73, Homography.kt:44

  • rectify accepts either winding. Corners given counter-clockwise on screen (TL, BL, BR, TR) produce a valid H that mirrors the texture (verified: accepted). Fix: require the clockwise image-space sign, or reorder the corners.
  • The DLT fixes h₃₃ = 1 in normalised coordinates. This fails if the source centroid maps to infinity. That cannot happen for convex→convex quads, but fromCorrespondences is public. Fix: document this, or add an SVD fallback.
  • det > 1e-300 is not scale-aware. Use a relative test.

Conditioning is fine: 4K-pixel corners give a max reprojection error of 9e-13 px.

MINOR-9: Display rounding chooses the step from the unrounded ±

Units.kt:135-156

A ± of 0.999 cm gives "45.5 cm ± 1.0", so the value shows 0.1 cm resolution against a ±1 cm range. stepFor sees 0.999, whose leading place is 0.1, and the ± is then rounded up to 1.0. Similarly, "0.02 m ± 0.020" shows the ± with more decimals than the value.

Fix. Round the ± to its displayed significant figures first, then derive the step from the rounded value.

Checked and correct: 6′ 0″ (no 5′ 12″) at 71.97 in and 71.99 in, 24 in at 23.97 in, negative values, -0 suppression, Hindi and German locales, and fraction reduction.

MINOR-10: The GLB is valid, with small polish items

GlbWriter.kt:149-152, GlbWriter.kt:164

The Khronos validator reports 0 errors and 0 warnings for textured, untextured and extreme-size boxes (0.1 mm × 50 m). It reports two infos:

  • UNUSED_OBJECT TEXCOORD_0 on the untextured primitive(s).
  • IMAGE_NPOT_DIMENSIONS when the JPEG is not a power of two. Homography.outputSize produces 1024×683 while the sampler asks for LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR.

Manually verified:

  • bufferView offsets are 4-aligned; there is no byteStride (tightly packed); the index accessor offset 12 is a multiple of 2; the image bufferView has no target.
  • POSITION has min/max and they match the floats exactly.
  • JSON padding is 0x20 and BIN padding is 0x00; all fields are little-endian.
  • All 12 triangles are counter-clockwise from outside (r × u = n for every face), so nothing is culled. UV (0,0) is top-left.

Suggestions:

  • Use a separate UV-less attribute set for the sides, or accept the info.
  • Either produce power-of-two textures or use minFilter LINEAR (9729), because Filament mip-maps NPOT textures but some viewers (Scene Viewer) may not.
  • Document that callers must bake JPEG EXIF orientation, since glTF ignores EXIF.

MINOR-12: Hot-path notes

These are not bugs.

  • PointEstimator is allocation-free per sample, and estimate() allocates only its four result objects. That is fine at 30 fps.
  • PlaneFitter.ransac performs up to 500 iterations × N points, and allocates BooleanArrays and list iterators. With 1–2k depth points that is several milliseconds, so it must run off the GL thread (Dispatchers.Default), not per frame. Say so in its KDoc.
  • Ray.fromPixel assumes the intrinsics and pixel coordinates are in the same (sensor/landscape) orientation. ARCore's getImageIntrinsics() is in sensor orientation, while touch coordinates are in display orientation. Document that the caller must convert with Frame.transformCoordinates2d.

M2 review: measure mode, engine fixes, M5 foundations (fbe1479 against f6ac685)

Reviewer: Reviewer agent, 2026-11-03. Scope: git diff f6ac685 fbe1479 (88 files). Source was not edited.

Verdict: 1 BLOCKER, 6 MAJOR, 17 MINOR. The ARCore plumbing is careful: every image, point cloud and raw-depth image is closed on every path, all ARCore reads happen on the frame thread, and the SceneView 4.65 calls match the jar. The problems are in what the ± means once it reaches the user. Its pixel units come from the screen rather than the camera, calibration leaks outside its scope, and the formatter rounds the ± down. BLOCKER

B1. The aim term uses screen pixels, so the ± depends on screen resolution and is several times too small. The tier floor is never applied.

Where

  • ar/.../MeasureSession.kt:270-272: fyView = projM[5] * viewH / 2.
  • MeasureSession.kt:605: LocalisationInput(range, s.focalPx, AIM_PIXELS).
  • MeasureSession.kt:540, 709: ADJUST_PIXELS = 1.0 view pixels.
  • engine/.../Uncertainty.kt:211-215, 233-238: the tier floor applies only to unlocalised endpoints.

What happens

  • The focal length passed to the engine is in view pixels: about 2000 on an FHD+ phone and about 2700 on a QHD+ phone. The engine's model, the research and the M0–M1 probe use camera-image pixels (Uncertainty.pixelLocalisationSigma: "≈2 mm at 1 m on a VGA stream, f≈500 px").
  • So "2 px" here is about 0.5 VGA pixel. The M0–M1 review measured the realistic aim error at about 1.5 VGA pixels (3 mm at 1 m). The modelled aim σ is therefore roughly 3× too small.
  • The aim σ also shrinks as the display gets sharper, although the camera is the same. A QHD+ phone shows a smaller ± than an FHD+ phone with the same sensor.
  • Every AR endpoint carries a LocalisationInput, so DeviceTier.absFloor95 (1 cm, the research rule "never better than ±1 cm on non-LiDAR Android") is never applied on the shipping path.

Failure scenario

  • A bank card at 30 cm shows "86 mm ± 2" with a GOOD badge (probe).
  • With the aim error the M0–M1 review measured, the real 95% band is about ±3–4 mm. Coverage drops back towards the 32–75% that review flagged as MAJOR-1.
  • The same mistake lets a card calibration at 30 cm pass: accepted with 0.61% when the focal is in view pixels, rejected as TOO_NOISY with a camera focal. The UI tells the user to hold the phone about 30 cm away (calib_card_detail), so most card calibrations will report a precision they do not have.
  • Freeze-frame makes it worse. The still comes from the CPU image (long side ≤ 1280, often 640×480), stretched about 3–4× onto the view. ADJUST_PIXELS = 1 view pixel is about ¼ of an image pixel, which no loupe can resolve.

Fix

  1. Express pixel error in camera pixels. Use the GPU-texture focal length (camera.textureIntrinsics.focalLength, the image the user actually sees) for the aim term. Use the CPU-image focal (camera.imageIntrinsics) for ADJUST_PIXELS, because the frozen still is the CPU image.
  2. Until the bench test proves smaller values, enforce tier.absFloor95 as a minimum on every point-to-point length, localised or not: σ_len ≥ absFloor95 / K95.
  3. Re-run the coverage scenarios with these units.

MAJOR

M1. The ± shown is rounded down, up to about 20% (decimal) and about 33% (fractional inches)

Where: engine/.../Units.kt:141, 147-151, 163-169, 268-272. decimalSteps and formatPlusMinus round the ±95 to the nearest displayed step; the inch path deliberately picks "the largest step not exceeding it".

Failure scenario

  • A 1.6 m room span on NO_DEPTH has a ±95 of 1.45 in. AUTO units format it as "5′ 3″ ± 1″" (probe).
  • A 60 cm object with ±2.49 cm shows "± 2".
  • The engine's coverage scenarios are tuned to just over 95% (minimum 95.05%), so every round-down pushes the band the user reads below 95%.

Fix

  • Round the ± up to the display step (ceil, with an epsilon for binary noise), then derive the value step from it. For inches, pick the smallest fraction step ≥ the ±.
  • Add a property test: displayed ± ≥ bound95 across contexts and preferences.

M2. A calibration still applies when the points are on different surfaces

Where

  • ar/.../MeasurementComposer.kt:204, 261-275: sharedPlane returns null when the points' planes differ.
  • engine/.../Calibration.kt:62-74 and Uncertainty.kt:201-204: "unknown plane (null) does not exclude".

Failure scenario: Calibrate with A4 on a table at 0.5 m, then measure floor → table-top height from about 0.6 m.

  • sharedPlane is null because the offset is 75 cm, so appliesTo(null, 0.6) is true.
  • The table's factor is applied: the value changes by 2.25 cm.
  • The ± drops from 15.1 mm to 5.1 mm (probe).
  • Floor → wall distances are treated as calibrated too.

The AR layer turns "points on different surfaces" into "plane unknown", which is exactly the case that should exclude. Research §2.9 says the factor corrects scale "for that plane and session".

Fix: In MeasurementComposer, when model.calibration?.plane != null and sharedPlane(...) == null, use model.withCalibration(null). Keep "unknown does not exclude" only for engine callers that truly lack geometry. Add tests for floor→table and floor→wall.

M3. A calibration from an earlier session is applied forever, to any plane, and claims to be tighter than no calibration

Where

  • app/.../MeasureViewModel.kt:63, 77-78, 117, 126-131
  • data/.../MeasureSettingsRepository.kt:52-56
  • MeasureScreen.kt:75, 204
  • AppNavHost.kt:110-118

What happens

  • Cross-session use. Calibrating from Home or onboarding goes through the CALIBRATE route, which has its own ViewModel. The calibration is therefore always cross-session when used in Measure.
  • Scope and floor across sessions. The plane scope is dropped and only the ±50% range scope remains. The floor is 0.8%, which is still below the NO_DEPTH prior of 1.02% σ.
  • No expiry and no way to clear it. A record never expires, and Settings has no "clear calibration" yet (TODO in HANDOFF).
  • Independent session scale errors. If VIO scale errors are independent between sessions (σ ≈ 1%), applying an old factor gives error s₂ − s₁. That has σ ≈ 1.4%, worse than not calibrating, while the ± shrinks.
  • Session id is the ViewModel's visit time, not the ARCore session. "Try again" after a session failure (sessionKey++), or a configuration change outside configChanges (locale, fontScale), creates a new ARCore world frame with the same id. The old plane and anchor, which are world coordinates, are then compared in the wrong frame, with the tight 0.3% floor. On a horizontal plane only the height matters. A new session starts at about the same phone height, so the stale plane can easily "match".

Fix

  • Until the bench test shows that scale carries over between sessions, apply a stored calibration only inside the ARCore session that produced it. Make the session id an ARCore-session identity: a counter bumped in onSessionCreated, held by MeasureSession, not by the ViewModel.
  • Otherwise show "Calibrated earlier, recalibrate" and use the uncalibrated model.
  • Add expiry, and add the Settings "clear calibration" toggle.
  • Only show the "Calibrated" chip (MeasureHud.kt:95-96) when the current reading is calibrated.

M4. The fitted surface overrides a nearer depth or feature hit and can land behind the object

Where

  • ar/.../MeasureSession.kt:591-603
  • LocalSurfaceModel.kt:101-113 (the cone accepts points at any depth along the ray)
  • FrameReaders.kt:78-82

What happens: FrameReaders.centreHit deliberately prefers a depth or feature hit that is clearly nearer than the ARCore plane (the box-on-floor case). The worker then discards that choice: for any non-PLANE hit it fits RANSAC to every cloud and depth point within the cone, whatever their depth, and takes the fitted surface first.

Failure scenario: Aim at the top of a 40 cm box on a textured floor from 1.2 m.

  • The cone (radius 8 cm) holds box-top and floor points.
  • If the floor supplies 60% or more of the inliers, the fit is the floor, and the endpoint is placed about 40 cm behind the box top.
  • planeBiasRel does not cover this, because it measures the fit's own noise, not choosing the wrong surface.
  • A frame hung 2–3 cm off a wall shows a smaller version of the same effect: a few % length bias.

Fix

  • Reject a fitted intersection whose range differs from the raw hit distance by more than about max(2 cm, 3%). Better, only feed points within a depth band around the raw hit into RANSAC.
  • Keep the fit only to refine the raw hit, never to replace it with a different depth layer.

M5. Anchors are detached while SceneView is pausing or closing the session on another thread

Where: ar/.../MeasureSession.kt:442-454, app/.../MeasureScreen.kt:76-77 against 147.

What happens

  • DisposableEffect(session) { close() } is registered before ARSceneView. Compose forgets effects in reverse order, so SceneView's dispose runs first.
  • ARCore.destroy() leads to ARSession.beginClose(). That calls onPaused on main, then hands Session.pause() and Session.close() to the SessionTeardown executor.
  • Next, on main, MeasureSession.close() calls anchor.detach() for every endpoint. Each call is nativeDetach(session.nativeWrapperHandle, …), a handle that close() does not zero. These calls therefore run concurrently with, or after, Session.close().
  • runCatching cannot catch a native fault.

Failure scenario: Place two points and press back. Depending on executor timing, the result is a native crash or undefined behaviour in ArAnchor_detach while the session is being destroyed. This is intermittent and only visible on a device.

Fix: Do not detach in close(), because Session.close() releases every anchor. Just drop the references. Alternatively, register the MeasureSession DisposableEffect after ARSceneView so it is disposed first, while the session is still alive. Add this to the device checklist: exit with points placed, 20 times.

M6. An interstitial can show on every exit from Measure, even with nothing saved, and it starts while the AR screen is still on screen

Where: app/.../nav/AppNavHost.kt:97-102, ads/AdsManager.kt:217-254, measure/MeasureScreen.kt:109-118.

What happens

  • close calls maybeShowAfterSave(BACK) whenever the Measure route is popped. That includes back from the permission or ARCore gate, and a visit with zero saves.
  • AdPolicy only counts lifetime scansSinceInterstitial. After the grace period, a user who saved 3 measurements yesterday opens Measure, cannot get tracking, presses back, and gets an interstitial.
  • That breaks the rule in docs/research/business.md and ui-ux §5.2: only after Save → Done, at a natural break. It also breaks the NextAction contract in AdPolicy.
  • ad.show() runs one DataStore read after popBackStack(). Navigation-compose's default 700 ms exit transition keeps the ARSceneView composed and drawing camera frames, so the ad opens over a still-visible AR screen.

Fix

  • Keep savedThisVisit in MeasureViewModel, set on Saved. Only request the ad when it is true.
  • Trigger the ad from the destination screen (Home or History) once the transition has settled and the AR entry is gone, for example a one-shot pendingInterstitial flag consumed by LaunchedEffect on Home.
  • Add an explicit "Done" on the Saved confirmation so NextAction.DONE is real.

M1. The TV diagonal ± is 20–27% too small, and it is shown with a ± even when the diagonal rests on a typed value

Where

  • app/.../gallery/GalleryFormatter.kt:62-68: diagonal() propagates σ_W and σ_H as independent.
  • GalleryFormatter.kt:79-80: diagonalDetail().
  • GalleryViewModels.kt:123-125: the diagonal row.

What happens

  • On every non-LiDAR tier the stored σ_W and σ_H are dominated by the same VIO scale error (σ_s ≈ 1%). The engine knows this. Shapes.rectangle (engine Shapes.kt:286-290) propagates only the random parts and then applies the scale once to the diagonal.
  • The gallery recombines the stored totals as if they were independent: σ_d = √((w σ_w)² + (h σ_h)²) / d. This cancels part of the correlated scale term.
  • For an estimated W or H, measurement() gives σ = 0. The diagonal then takes the ± of the other side only. The row shows, for example, "43.0 in ± 0.3 (109 cm)" and "Estimated (typed, not measured)". A typed value ends up carrying a measured-looking ±, which is exactly what the edit rules forbid.

Failure scenario (NO_DEPTH, σ_s = 1.02%, 2 mm random per side; ±95)

Item Engine Gallery shows Under by
43″ TV, 0.952 × 0.536 m ±22.2 mm (0.87 in) ±17.8 mm (0.70 in) 20%
4:3 monitor, 0.60 × 0.45 m ±15.5 mm ±11.7 mm 25%
Square panel ±14.7 mm ±10.7 mm 27%

The M2 review (M1) already established that the engine's coverage is tuned to just over 95%, so a 20% shrink drops the band the user reads well below 95%.

In the second case, the user edits the TV width to the box label's 95 cm. The spec block still shows the diagonal "± 0.3 in", which is no longer a measurement of anything.

Fix

  • Carry the engine's diagonal in the contract. Add diagonal: Dimension? to ScanDraft and a diagonal_bound95_m column. v2 has not shipped, so 2.json can still change without a migration.
  • On edit, recompute it conservatively: bound95_d = (w·b_w + h·b_h) / d. This is the fully-correlated bound, ≥ the engine value in all three cases above (22.5 / 16.0 / 15.2 mm).
  • When W or H is an estimate, show the diagonal without a ± (σ = 0, "~"), as diagonalInches already does on the card.
  • Add a test: when either side is an estimate, the diagonal row has no plusMinus; when neither is, the diagonal ± ≥ the fully-correlated bound.

M2. updateDimensions writes the new GLB before the database row, so the model and the row can disagree

Where: data/.../gallery/GalleryRepository.kt:205-219. writeAtomic(model, glb) (210) replaces model.glb in place, then dao.setDimensions(...) (211-219) runs outside any transaction. The KDoc (186-191) promises "the item is then unchanged" on failure.

  • setDimensions throws (SQLiteFull, I/O error, or the DB closed on a corrupt file). guarded returns false and the user sees "Couldn't save the change".

M3. The gallery-item route uses up the after-save interstitial, so Place on the Saved screen will show an ad

What happens

  • NonArScreenAdEffect makes any non-AR destination that reaches RESUMED use up a pending after-measure chance, then show the interstitial if AdPolicy allows it.

BLOCKER

B1. The front photo is never taken: repeated frames reset the hold every other call

Where

  • ar/.../FrontCaptureGate.kt:108-117: if (prev == null || dt <= 0 || dt > MAX_GAP_S) { angular = NaN; linear = NaN }.
  • FrontCaptureGate.kt:88,91-93: !isSteady() gives HOLD_STILL, which sets holdingSinceNs = -1.
  • ar/.../MeasureSession.kt:400,740-752: the gate is fed on every onFrame, with no timestamp check.

What happens

  • onSessionUpdated runs once per display vsync, and in LATEST_CAMERA_IMAGE mode a call without a new camera image returns the same Frame timestamp (see above).
  • On each repeated frame dt == 0, so the motion estimate becomes NaN. The gate then reports HOLD_STILL and restarts the 600 ms hold.
  • The next real frame starts the hold again, and the repeat after it resets it. held >= HOLD_NS is never reached.

Failure scenario

  • On any phone with a 60/90/120 Hz display, step 3 never captures. The probe shows this over 10 s of a perfectly still, parallel hold.
  • The card flickers between "Hold still." and "Hold still. Taking the photo…" about 30 times a second, which also makes frontCapture emit every frame.
  • The text is in a polite live region, so TalkBack keeps re-announcing it.
  • The user's only way forward is Skip photo, so no scan ever gets its front photo, which is the core of M3. The existing tests feed strictly increasing timestamps, so they pass.

Fix

  1. In MeasureSession.processFrontCapture, skip the gate when frame.timestamp == lastCaptureTs, and remember the last timestamp. This also halves the per-frame work.
  2. In FrontCaptureGate.updateMotion, treat dt <= 1e-4 like TrackingGate does: return without touching angular, linear, lastTs, lastPose or the hold.
  3. Add a test: a 30 fps camera fed at 60 Hz and at 120 Hz, with each timestamp repeated, captures within HOLD_NS + 3 camera frames.
  4. Add to the device checklist: "photo is auto-taken within about 1 s of holding still, on a 90/120 Hz phone".

MAJOR

M1. For a box, the photographed face is chosen separately from the outline's W/D. The rule picks the short side while the user stands in front of the long face.

Where

  • app/.../scan/ScanArScreen.kt:272: remember(finished) { outlineOf(finished) } → MeasureSession.scanOutline (:514-518). This decides W vs D with frontEdge(points, pose at that moment) (ScanComposer.kt:90-93).
  • MeasureSession.kt:531: startFrontCapture recomputes captureEdge with the pose at arming time, and that edge picks the photographed face (ScanComposer.kt:104-110,153-165).
  • ScanComposer.kt:116-129: frontEdge = the base edge whose midpoint is horizontally nearest the camera.
  • ScanViewModel.kt:142: the photo is warped to outline.width / outline.height.
  • Same root cause for a horizontal flat face (a poster on a table): faceUp (ScanComposer.kt:173-178) takes "up" from the camera direction once at outline time and again at every face refresh. W/H is fixed at outline time, but the photo's orientation follows wherever the user stands at capture.

What happens

  1. The edge-midpoint rule is wrong for long objects. In the probe, standing 0.4–0.5 m in front of a 2 m wardrobe, 10–20 cm inside its right end, selects the 0.6 m side.
  2. The decision is made twice, at different moments: when the reading finishes (and again whenever the reading changes as anchors refine), and when the photo step arms.
  3. If the two decisions differ:
    • The outline says W = front edge A.
    • The capture photographs face B, of width D.
    • The view model stretches face B's photo to A's W:H.
    • The gallery GLB puts the side panel on the front.

Failure scenario

  • A user taps the top of a 120 × 40 cm cabinet while standing near its front-right corner, which is the natural place to see the top.
  • The Outline card shows W 120, D 40.
  • After Next, startFrontCapture picks the side edge. The guidance says "Turn to face the object" until the user faces the 40 cm side panel.
  • The 40 × 85 cm panel is then warped into a 120 × 85 texture, a 3× horizontal stretch, and saved without warning.
  • If the user doesn't move, the Outline card can also swap W and D while it is on screen, because the outline is recomputed for each new reading.

Fix

  • Decide the front face once, when the user taps Next. Store it in ScanOutline (frontEdge: Int, and for FLAT the up vector used) and pass it to startFrontCapture(shape, outline). The capture then never re-decides.
  • Choose the edge by facing, not by midpoint distance. For example, take argmax over k of n_k · normalize(horizontal(camera − mid_k)), where n_k is the outward horizontal normal of edge k. That is the face the camera is most squarely in front of.
  • Tests:
    • the probe positions, which should all give edge 0;
    • an outline/capture round trip where the camera moves between Next and arming, and W/D and the face must not change;
    • a horizontal flat face captured from the other side, which must keep the W/H orientation.

M2. After a pause, the photo is taken against frozen, unanchored corners

Where

  • MeasureSession.kt:321-325: onSessionPaused detaches every anchor. A complete outline keeps its last positions, unanchored, by M2 design.
  • MeasureSession.kt:385-400: unanchored points are skipped, so allTracking stays true.
  • MeasureSession.kt:527-539: startFrontCapture does not check for unanchored points.
  • ScanArScreen.kt:142-148: the photo step re-arms automatically once sessionRunning is true again.

What happens

  • M2 deliberately treats points left by a pause as frozen. freeze() refuses them (:555) and undo() clears them all (:460), because ARCore may not relocalise to the same world frame on resume.
  • The front capture ignores this. It projects those frozen world positions into the post-resume camera and gives "step back / turn" guidance towards wherever they now land.
  • trackingOk is true, so it captures and saves whatever the camera sees there.

Failure scenario

  • In step 3 the user presses the power button or takes a call, then comes back. Pausing to grab a tape measure is natural in a scan flow.
  • ARCore resumes with a shifted origin. The capture now guides the user to a patch of wall 30 cm to the left of the frame, and auto-captures it.
  • The Review shows a straightened photo of plain wall, which is then saved as the item's front.
  • The same happens if the pause occurs on the Outline step and the user taps Next afterwards.

Fix

  • In startFrontCapture (and in processFrontCapture), refuse when any endpoint has anchor == null. Expose this as a hint, for example CaptureHint.REMEASURE.
  • In ScanArScreen, show "The outline was lost when the app paused. Measure again or skip the photo", with [Measure again] (Undo clears all) and [Skip photo].
  • Test this with a fake: mark the endpoints unanchored and assert that the capture never arms.
  • Add to the device checklist: "pause in step 3 (power button), resume → the photo is refused, not taken".
